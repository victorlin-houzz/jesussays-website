Bible Verses About Faith and Trust in God

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

The Bible describes faith as confidence in God’s word and character before you can see how things will turn out (Hebrews 11:1). Trust is that faith put to work: leaning on God instead of your own understanding (Proverbs 3:5-6), telling him what you fear, and taking the next faithful step. Scripture never says you have to stop feeling afraid first.

What does the Bible say about faith and trust in God?

These six verses show what faith is, where it comes from, and what it looks like on an ordinary, anxious day.

  1. Hebrews 11:1 — “Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.” “Substance” suggests something solid to stand on. Faith isn’t wishful thinking. It rests on what God has already said while the outcome is still out of view.
  2. Proverbs 3:5-6 — “Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.” Your understanding matters, but it can’t carry your full weight. To acknowledge God “in all thy ways” means bringing him into the actual decisions, including the practical ones.
  3. Psalm 56:3 — “What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee.” David doesn’t claim he’ll never be afraid. He decides ahead of time what he will do when fear comes. It’s a prayer you can pray in one breath.
  4. Isaiah 26:3 — “Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.” A mind “stayed” on God is one that leans on him and keeps coming back to rest there. Peace follows where your attention settles.
  5. Romans 10:17 — “So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.” You can’t manufacture faith by effort. It grows as you keep hearing and reading what God has said, which is why a little Scripture every day matters.
  6. Mark 9:24 — “Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.” A desperate father admitted that belief and unbelief were both in him. Jesus didn’t send him away to sort himself out. He healed the man’s son. Weak faith brought to Jesus is still faith.

If your faith feels thin right now, you may find it helpful to read our Christian advice for seasons of doubt.

What is the difference between faith and trust?

In the Bible the two overlap so much that they often describe the same thing. The New Testament word usually translated “faith” can also mean trust or faithfulness. One simple way to hold them together: faith believes God is who he says he is, and trust puts your weight on that belief. You can say a chair will hold you; trust is sitting down. Hebrews 11:6 joins the two when it says that anyone who comes to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who diligently seek him. Believing and relying belong together.

How do I trust God when I feel out of control?

  1. Name what you’re holding onto. A vague dread is hard to hand over. Write down the specific outcome you’re trying to control: the test result, the job offer, your child’s choices.
  2. Pour it out in prayer. Psalm 62:8 invites you to pour out your heart before God. Tell him plainly what you want and what you fear, then pray Proverbs 3:5-6 over that one thing.
  3. Take the next faithful step. Trust usually shows up as obedience before it shows up as a feeling. Do the next wise, right thing in front of you, even while you’re still anxious.
  4. Remember what God has done. Write down two or three times God carried you, or someone you love, through a hard season. Israel was told again and again to remember, because memory feeds trust.
  5. Don’t carry it alone. Hebrews 10:25 urges believers not to stop gathering together. Tell a pastor or trusted friend what you’re facing and let them pray with you.

If fear is crowding out your days or keeping you up at night, these Bible verses about peace and fear may help, and it’s wise to talk with a doctor or counselor as well.

Does faith mean God will give me what I ask for?

No. Faith trusts God himself, not a particular outcome. Presumption decides what God must do and then calls that faith. The clearest picture in Scripture comes from three young men facing a furnace in Babylon:

If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.

Daniel 3:17-18

They were sure God was able. They weren’t sure he would do it their way. “But if not” is where mature faith lives. It asks boldly, leaves the answer to God’s wisdom, and keeps trusting his goodness when the answer is no or not yet. That kind of faith is harder to hold, and it holds up far better when life doesn’t go to plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I increase my faith?

Ask God for it, as the apostles did when they asked Jesus to increase their faith (Luke 17:5). Then put yourself where faith tends to grow: regular time in Scripture, honest prayer, worship with other believers, and small acts of obedience. Faith usually grows slowly through daily habits rather than through one dramatic moment, so be patient with yourself.

How do you trust God when prayers seem unanswered?

Hold onto what you know about God’s character while you wait for what you don’t yet know about the outcome. Many psalms were written in that gap, and they keep speaking to God instead of giving up on him. Keep praying honestly, keep reading, and ask a mature believer to pray with you. Silence is not proof that God has stopped caring.

What does it mean to walk by faith, not by sight?

In 2 Corinthians 5:7, Paul is writing about living in this body while longing to be with the Lord. Walking by faith means shaping your life now around what God has promised about the future, including the resurrection, rather than only what you can see today. It affects everyday choices: how you handle loss, money, fear, and hope.

Why does God allow our faith to be tested?

James 1:2-4 says the trying of faith produces patience, and 1 Peter 1:6-7 compares tested faith to gold refined by fire. That doesn’t mean God sends every hardship or that pain is good in itself. It means hard seasons are not wasted. God can use them to deepen a faith that had never been tried and to show it is genuine.

Is faith the same as positive thinking?

No. Positive thinking expects things to turn out well, while biblical faith trusts God whether they do or not. Faith can be honest about grief, pain, and fear, as the Psalms are. Its confidence rests on who God is, not on predicting good outcomes, which is why faith can survive a hard diagnosis or a loss that optimism can’t explain.
