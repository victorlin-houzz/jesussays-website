A Prayer for Depression and Hopelessness

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

A prayer for depression and hopelessness can be as simple as “Lord, I can’t hold on right now, so please hold on to me.” God doesn’t need polished words, and depression is not proof that your faith has failed. The Bible gives honest language for despair, and prayer can go hand in hand with rest, trusted people, and professional care.

Care note: Prayer and Scripture can steady you, but they are not a substitute for a doctor, counselor, pastor, or emergency help. If you are thinking about ending your life or can’t keep yourself safe, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) now if you are in the US. Outside the US, contact your local emergency services. Then tell someone near you what is going on.

A prayer for when you feel depressed and hopeless

Pray this slowly, aloud or in your head. Change any words that don’t fit. If you can only manage the first line today, that is enough.

Lord, I won’t pretend I’m okay. I’m tired in a way sleep doesn’t fix, and I can’t see a way forward. You found Elijah under the juniper tree when he wanted to give up. You heard David when he asked how long you would hide your face. You know exactly where I am right now.

I’m not asking you to make me feel better all at once. I’m asking you to stay. When my thoughts turn dark, guard my mind. When I’m sure no one cares, remind me that you do, and bring someone near. Give me strength for the next small thing: to eat, to rest, to answer one message, to ask for help.

I can’t feel hope today, so I’m borrowing it from your Word. Your compassions fail not, and they are new every morning. Hold on to me when I can’t hold on to you. In Jesus’ name, amen.

How do I pray when I’m too depressed to pray?

Depression often drains the strength and focus that prayer seems to require. Paul wrote that God makes room for exactly that weakness:

Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.

Romans 8:26

“Infirmities” means weaknesses. Your sighs and silences are not wasted. These steps are small on purpose:

  1. Pray one sentence. “Lord, I’m here.” “Help me.” Repeat it as often as you need to. Short prayers are real prayers.
  2. Borrow a psalm. Read Psalm 13 or Psalm 88 aloud as your own words. They were kept in Scripture for people who can’t find their own.
  3. Let someone else pray for you. Tell a friend, pastor, or priest, “I’m struggling. Will you pray for me?” You don’t have to carry the whole work of praying alone.
  4. Pray with your body. Sit outside, open your hands, or kneel for a minute. When words won’t come, posture can still say, “I’m turning toward you.”
  5. Keep the bar low. In a dark season, faithfulness might look like two honest minutes a day. That counts.

Is it okay to tell God I feel hopeless or angry?

Yes. The Bible keeps these prayers on purpose. David didn’t hide his despair; he asked God about it directly:

How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me? How long shall I take counsel in my soul, having sorrow in my heart daily?

Psalm 13:1-2

Psalm 88 goes further and ends in darkness, with no tidy resolution. Jeremiah cursed the day he was born (Jeremiah 20:14). And in Gethsemane Jesus told his friends, “My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me” (Matthew 26:38). Notice what he did with that sorrow. He said it out loud, asked his friends to stay close, and kept praying. A complaint brought to God is still prayer. It’s a way of refusing to let go of him.

What if I don’t feel God when I pray?

Numbness is one of depression’s cruelest symptoms. It can mute the very comfort you’re reaching for. Feeling far from God is not the same as being far from him. On the cross Jesus prayed the opening line of Psalm 22, “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” (Matthew 27:46). The Son of God knows what it is to pray into silence.

The writer of Lamentations, sitting in the ruins of Jerusalem, shows another way through. He didn’t wait until he felt hopeful. He chose what to remember:

This I recall to my mind, therefore have I hope. It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:21-23

“This I recall to my mind” is something you can do even when your feelings are flat. Keep showing up. Feelings often return later and slowly, and God is present before they do.

Does getting help mean my prayers aren’t working?

No. When Elijah collapsed in the wilderness and asked God to take his life, the first answer wasn’t a rebuke or a sermon. An angel woke him with food and water, let him sleep, and came back to do it again:

And the angel of the LORD came again the second time, and touched him, and said, Arise and eat; because the journey is too great for thee.

1 Kings 19:7

God cared for Elijah’s body before he spoke to him about anything else. Sleep, meals, a walk, a doctor’s visit, counseling, and treatment your doctor recommends can all be part of how God answers your prayer. If the heaviness hangs on for weeks or makes ordinary life hard, talk with a doctor or licensed counselor. Asking for help is a faithful step. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a short prayer for depression?

Try this: “Lord, I’m worn out and I can’t feel you. Stay with me today, give me strength for one next step, and bring someone near. Amen.” A prayer that short can be prayed on waking, in the car, or before a hard conversation. Write it on a card by your bed so you don’t have to find words when you have none. God hears brief, honest prayers as fully as long ones.

Can Christians take antidepressants?

Yes. Nothing in Scripture forbids medicine, and the New Testament speaks warmly of physicians; Paul calls Luke “the beloved physician” (Colossians 4:14). Whether medication is right for you is a decision to make with a doctor who knows your history. Don’t start or stop a medication on your own. Many believers pray for their treatment, their doctors, and their recovery all at once.

How can I pray for a friend who is depressed?

Pray for their safety, rest, and good care, and for people to surround them. Tell them you’re praying without adding pressure to feel better or to “have more faith.” Then pray with your hands too: bring a meal, drive them to an appointment, or sit with them. If they mention wanting to die, stay with them and help them call or text 988 in the US.

What should I do if I feel like giving up?

Tell someone right now: a friend, family member, or pastor. In the US, call or text 988 to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; if you are in immediate danger, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. Outside the US, contact local emergency services. Put distance between yourself and anything you could use to hurt yourself. Your one prayer tonight can be, “God, keep me safe until morning.”

Will God take my depression away?

He may, and it is right to ask him. Relief sometimes comes gradually, through treatment, rest, relationships, and time, and some faithful people carry heaviness for a long season. Scripture doesn’t give a timeline. It does describe believers as “cast down, but not destroyed” (2 Corinthians 4:9). Keep asking, keep accepting help, and let others hold hope for you when you can’t.
