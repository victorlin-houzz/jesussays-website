What Would Jesus Say About Your Purpose?

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Jesus would say your purpose is to follow him, love God with all your heart, and love your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 4:19; 22:37–39). He calls people to himself first, then sends them to serve and bear fruit that lasts. For most people that calling takes shape in ordinary places, one faithful day at a time.

What did Jesus say about purpose?

Jesus spoke often about what our lives are for. These are some of his clearest words on it.

  1. Matthew 4:19 — “Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men.” Jesus called Peter and Andrew while they were at work, casting nets into the sea. The call came before any job description. Purpose begins with a person to follow, and what you become (“I will make you”) is his work in you.
  2. Matthew 22:37-39 — “Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.” Asked what matters most, Jesus gave two commands. Whatever else your calling includes, it will fit inside these.
  3. John 15:5 — “I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.” Fruit grows from staying connected. To abide is to remain with Jesus in prayer, in his words, and in obedience, and a fruitful life follows from that.
  4. Mark 10:45 — “For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.” Jesus said this to disciples arguing over who was greatest. “Minister” means serve, as the Greek verb in Mark 10:45 shows. In his kingdom, greatness is measured by service.
  5. Mark 5:19 — “Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.” A man Jesus had set free begged to travel with him. Jesus sent him home instead. For many people, the calling is right where they already live.

Does God have a specific purpose for my life?

Yes, though it may not look like a secret plan you have to decode. It helps to think of calling in two layers. First is the calling Jesus gives every disciple: follow him, love God, love your neighbor, and make disciples. Second is how that calling takes shape in your life, through your gifts, family, work, neighborhood, and season.

Paul writes that believers are God’s workmanship, created for good works that God prepared beforehand (Ephesians 2:10). Those works are usually found by walking, not by waiting. Jesus called his disciples one by one, by name, and he rarely gave anyone more than the next step.

How do I find my purpose as a Christian?

  1. Start with what Jesus has already made clear. Love the people in front of you, forgive, tell the truth, serve. Clarity about the bigger picture often comes while you’re doing what you already know.
  2. Pray for wisdom, and keep asking. James 1:5 promises that God gives wisdom generously to those who ask. Make it a daily prayer, not a one-time request.
  3. Notice your gifts and burdens. Write down what you do well, what others thank you for, and which needs move you most. Romans 12:6-8 and 1 Peter 4:10 teach that gifts are given to serve others. In the parable of the talents, the master praises servants who were faithful with the little they were given (Matthew 25:21).
  4. Serve before you feel certain. Volunteer, help a neighbor, offer your skills to your church. Many people discover their calling by showing up, then noticing where they come alive and where others are helped.
  5. Ask people who know you. Proverbs 15:22 says purposes are established with many counselors. A pastor, mentor, or friend in faith can often see gifts you overlook.

What if my purpose feels small, or I’ve failed?

Jesus spent most of his life in Nazareth, working and living among family and neighbors, before his public ministry began when he was about thirty (Luke 3:23). If your days feel ordinary, you’re in good company. Faithfulness in small, unseen things matters to God.

Comparison can quietly steal your sense of calling. After the resurrection, Peter pointed at John and asked what would happen to him. Jesus answered:

If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee? follow thou me.

John 21:22

Someone else’s path isn’t your assignment. And that same conversation in John 21 began with failure. Peter had denied Jesus three times, and Jesus didn’t set him aside. He asked Peter three times whether he loved him, and each time gave him work to do: “Feed my sheep” (John 21:17).

Failure doesn’t disqualify you from God’s purposes. Confession and grace bring you back to the one who calls you. If a specific failure is weighing on you, this confession prayer after failure may help.

A prayer for purpose and direction

Lord Jesus, I want to follow you. I don’t always know what you made me for, but I know you’ve called me to love God and to love the people in front of me. Show me the next faithful step and give me courage to take it.

Keep me close to you so my life bears fruit that lasts. Where I’ve failed, restore me. Where I’m comparing, turn my eyes back to you. Amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the purpose of life according to Jesus?

In his prayer the night before he died, Jesus said eternal life is to know the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he sent (John 17:3). Knowing God sits at the center, and from it flow love for God and neighbor, service, and fruitfulness. In Jesus’ teaching, purpose is mainly about relationship: being with him and becoming like him wherever you are.

Is my job my calling?

Your job can be part of your calling, but your calling is bigger than any job. Colossians 3:23 tells believers to work heartily “as to the Lord,” which gives dignity to every honest kind of work, from caring for children to running a business. If you lose a job or change careers, your deeper calling to follow Jesus and love others stays the same.

Is Jeremiah 29:11 about my purpose?

Jeremiah 29:11 was written to Judah’s exiles in Babylon, who were told they would stay there seventy years before God brought them home (Jeremiah 29:10). It shows God’s faithful intentions toward his people, even through long hardship. It reveals his character, but it isn’t a personal promise of a particular career or an easy life. Read it with the whole chapter.

How do I know God’s will for my life?

Start with what God has already made clear in Scripture, because much of his will is revealed there: love, holiness, justice, mercy, prayer. For choices the Bible doesn’t address directly, pray for wisdom, seek counsel from mature believers, and weigh your gifts and circumstances. Romans 12:2 links a renewed mind with discerning God’s will. Often his guidance is clearest looking back.

What if I feel like I have no purpose at all?

Feeling purposeless often follows a loss, a move, retirement, or a long season of unseen work. Tell God honestly how empty it feels, and take one small step of love or service today. If the emptiness comes with hopelessness, constant sadness, or thoughts of not wanting to live, please talk to a doctor or counselor, and in the US call or text 988.
