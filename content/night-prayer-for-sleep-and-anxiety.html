Night Prayer for Sleep and Anxiety

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

A night prayer for sleep and anxiety is a short, honest handing-over: you name what your mind is still holding, give it to God, and ask him to keep you while you rest. You don't need special words. The prayer below borrows from Psalm 4:8 and Psalm 31:5, lines Christians have prayed at bedtime for centuries.

Care note: Prayer can quiet a restless mind, but it doesn't replace care. If anxiety or sleeplessness goes on for weeks, or you find yourself relying on alcohol or pills to sleep, talk with a doctor or counselor. If your thoughts turn to harming yourself, call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the US, or contact local emergency services.

What is a good night prayer for sleep and anxiety?

Pray this slowly, out loud if you can or in a whisper if you can't. Where there's a blank, name the actual things.

Lord, the day is over, and I can't carry it any further. You know what my mind keeps returning to: ______. I set each one down in front of you.

What I left undone, I leave with you. Where I did wrong, I confess it and receive your mercy. What frightens me about tomorrow, I give you tonight, because tomorrow belongs to you too.

You never slumber or sleep, so I don't have to stay awake to keep watch. Into thine hand I commit my spirit. I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety. Amen.

If you wake in the night, you don't have to start over. Pray just the last two lines.

What Bible verses help you sleep when you're anxious?

Psalm 4:8, the last line of the prayer above, is the classic bedtime verse. These four belong beside it.

  • Psalm 3:5 — “I laid me down and slept; I awaked; for the LORD sustained me.” The psalm's heading places David on the run from his own son Absalom. He slept while the danger was still real, because he trusted who was holding him.
  • Psalm 121:3–4 — “He will not suffer thy foot to be moved: he that keepeth thee will not slumber. Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.” This is one of the Songs of Degrees, traditionally sung by pilgrims on the road to Jerusalem. You can close your eyes because Someone else is keeping watch.
  • Psalm 127:2 — “It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep.” Staying up late to worry can't do what only God can do. This isn't a promise that sleep will always come easily, and a hard night isn't a sign God is displeased. It's an invitation to stop trying to earn rest by worrying longer.
  • Psalm 139:11–12 — “If I say, Surely the darkness shall cover me; even the night shall be light about me. Yea, the darkness hideth not from thee; but the night shineth as the day: the darkness and the light are both alike to thee.” Problems often look bigger at 3 a.m. To God, that hour looks the same as noon.

How do I pray when I can't sleep?

  1. Unload before you lie down. Write each worry on paper, one line apiece, and pray over the list item by item. Leave the paper outside the bedroom if you can. It will still be there in the morning; you don't need to guard it overnight. If a guided pause helps, sit through a quiet minute with Still Waters, then put the phone across the room.
  2. Pray one verse, not five. Choose one line from above and say it slowly a few times. It gives a busy mind one simple thing to hold instead of a dozen things to solve.
  3. Keep middle-of-the-night prayers short. When you wake with your mind racing, don't argue with the worry or start planning tomorrow. Breathe slowly and pray one line, such as “Into thine hand I commit my spirit.” If you're still wide awake after a while, it's fine to get up, sit somewhere dim, and read a psalm until you feel drowsy.
  4. Pray for someone else. Anxious minds turn inward at night. Praying for a friend in the hospital, your children, or a coworker who is struggling turns your attention outward and places your worries in God's hands alongside theirs.
  5. In the morning, look back. Glance at last night's list. Some worries will look smaller in daylight; some will need action today. Either way, you made it through the night, and God kept you.

Is it okay to tell God I'm too anxious to sleep?

Yes. The Psalms are full of sleepless prayers, and God doesn't need yours to be polished. One psalmist says it plainly:

Thou holdest mine eyes waking: I am so troubled that I cannot speak.

Psalm 77:4

Notice what the psalmist does next. He doesn't pretend to feel better. He decides to remember what God has done before (Psalm 77:11), and the rest of Psalm 77 slowly turns from complaint toward trust. You can pray the same way: tell God the truth about tonight, then remind yourself of one time he carried you. For Scripture on worry during the day, see these Bible verses for anxiety and worry.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it wrong to fall asleep while praying?

No. Falling asleep while you talk with God isn't disrespect; it's the rest you were asking for. Children fall asleep mid-sentence with parents they trust, and God is a far better Father. If you want more focused, wakeful prayer, set aside time earlier in the day, and let your bedtime prayer be short and simple.

What psalms are traditionally prayed at night?

Night Prayer, or Compline, prayed in Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, and other traditions, has long used Psalm 4, Psalm 91, and Psalm 134, along with the words of Psalm 31:5. Psalm 91 speaks of God's shelter. It's a prayer of trust, not a guarantee that nothing bad can happen, and it's best prayed that way.

Does God care about my sleep?

Yes. God made human bodies to need rest and built a weekly Sabbath into his law. Even Jesus slept, on a pillow in the back of a fishing boat, in the middle of a storm (Mark 4:38). Caring for your sleep, whether that means an earlier bedtime, fewer late-night screens, or a conversation with your doctor, is part of looking after the life God gave you.

What should I do if anxiety wakes me up every night?

An occasional bad night is part of life. If you wake with anxiety most nights for several weeks, mention it to your doctor; persistent sleep trouble is worth a medical conversation. In the meantime, keep your night prayer short and the same each time, and bring the bigger worries to God, and to someone you trust, during the day.

Can I pray lying down?

Yes. Scripture shows people praying standing, kneeling, sitting, and lying in bed. Psalm 63:6 speaks of remembering God “upon my bed” and meditating on him “in the night watches.” God listens to the heart, not the posture. If lying down helps your body relax, that is a fine way to pray before sleep.
