Bible Verses for Healing

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Bible verses for healing include Jeremiah 17:14, Psalm 147:3, Psalm 103:2-3, Psalm 41:3, Isaiah 53:5, and Revelation 21:4. They invite you to ask God for healing and assure you of his care in sickness. They are not guarantees of a cure on a set timeline, and they never suggest that people who stay sick lacked faith.

Care note: Scripture can comfort you through illness, but it is no substitute for medical care. See a doctor about symptoms, keep up the treatment you’ve been given, and call 911 or your local emergency number in an emergency. If a diagnosis or long illness has pushed you toward despair or thoughts of suicide, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the US, or reach local emergency services if you are outside the US.

What are the most comforting Bible verses for healing?

The verses below are quoted from the King James Version, with a note on what each one means in its setting.

  1. Jeremiah 17:14 — “Heal me, O LORD, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved: for thou art my praise.” Jeremiah prayed this while he was mocked and opposed, so his “heal me” reaches past the body to the whole person. You can pray it word for word.
  2. Psalm 147:3 — “He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.” The picture is of someone carefully dressing a wound. God’s care covers grief and heartbreak as well as disease.
  3. Psalm 103:2-3 — “Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits: Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases.” David praises God as the source of every healing. The same psalm admits that “his days are as grass” (verse 15), so it tells you where healing comes from rather than promising that believers never get sick.
  4. Psalm 41:3 — “The LORD will strengthen him upon the bed of languishing: thou wilt make all his bed in his sickness.” “Languishing” means lying weak. The image is tender: God attending a sickbed and making it comfortable. It speaks of his care during an illness, not of a date when it will end.
  5. Isaiah 53:5 — “But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.” “Stripes” are the wounds of a whip. Matthew connects this chapter to Jesus healing the sick (Matthew 8:17), and Peter applies it to forgiveness of sin (1 Peter 2:24). Every prayer for healing rests on what Christ suffered.
  6. 2 Corinthians 4:16 — “For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day.” Paul is frank that bodies wear out. For chronic illness or aging, he offers something that can be renewed even then.
  7. Revelation 21:4 — “And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.” This is the healing Scripture promises to all God’s people without exception: complete and final, when he makes all things new.

Does the Bible promise that God will heal every sickness?

Not in this life. Jesus healed many people, and Christians still pray for healing and thank God when it comes, whether suddenly or through time and medicine. But the New Testament is honest that faithful believers stayed sick.

Paul left his coworker Trophimus “at Miletum sick” (2 Timothy 4:20). Timothy had “often infirmities” (1 Timothy 5:23). Epaphroditus nearly died before he recovered (Philippians 2:27). Paul asked three times for his own “thorn in the flesh” to be removed, and God answered with grace instead (2 Corinthians 12:7-9).

So the Bible holds two things together. God is able to heal and invites us to ask. The full healing of our bodies is promised at the resurrection. Verses like Psalm 103:3 tell you who heals; they don’t give you a date.

Is sickness a punishment for sin or a lack of faith?

People who ask this have often heard it from someone else. When Jesus’ disciples saw a man born blind, they asked whose sin had caused it. Jesus answered:

Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him.

John 9:3

Scripture sometimes links a particular wrong with its consequences, and James mentions confession alongside prayer for the sick. But the Bible refuses to make the link automatic. Job’s friends insisted his suffering proved his guilt, and God said they were wrong. If you’ve been told you are still sick because you didn’t believe enough, you can set that burden down. Faith is trust in God. It is not a force that guarantees an outcome.

Is it faithless to see a doctor?

No. Jesus used the doctor as a picture of his own mission: “They that are whole need not a physician; but they that are sick” (Luke 5:31). One of Paul’s traveling companions was Luke, a doctor Paul called “the beloved physician” (Colossians 4:14). Paul told Timothy to take a little wine for his stomach. When God promised King Hezekiah that he would recover, Isaiah still had a poultice of figs laid on the boil (Isaiah 38:21).

Healing that comes through medicine, surgery, therapy, and rest still comes from God. Pray for your care team, follow their advice, and ask questions when you don’t understand. If you want words to bring to God, try this prayer for healing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best Bible verse for healing?

The best Bible verse for healing depends on what you are facing. To ask God directly, pray Jeremiah 17:14 or Psalm 6:2. During a hospital stay, Psalm 41:3 pictures God at the bedside. For heartbreak, Psalm 147:3 fits; for chronic illness, 2 Corinthians 4:16 speaks to a body wearing down. After a loss, Revelation 21:4 holds out final healing. Choose one and pray it daily.

Does “with his stripes we are healed” mean physical healing?

Christians answer this differently. Matthew 8:17 ties Isaiah 53 to Jesus healing the sick, and 1 Peter 2:24 applies it to sin: “by whose stripes ye were healed.” Many read it as grounding all healing, body and soul, in the cross, with complete physical healing at the resurrection. It is not a promise that every illness will be cured now.

What does Jehovah Rapha mean?

Jehovah Rapha comes from Exodus 15:26, where God says, “I am the LORD that healeth thee.” The Hebrew behind that phrase is often written Jehovah Rapha or Yahweh Rapha. God spoke it to Israel at Marah, after making bitter water sweet, as part of his covenant with them. It reveals his character as healer. Like other healing verses, it tells us who God is rather than guaranteeing a cure on our timeline.

What Bible verse can I send to someone in the hospital?

Choose something short and gentle. The priestly blessing works well: “The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee” (Numbers 6:24-25). Add a personal note, say you are praying, and offer something practical, like a meal for their family. Avoid promising that they will be healed; just assure them of God’s care and yours.

What did Jesus mean by “thy faith hath made thee whole”?

Jesus said this to a woman who had been ill for twelve years and reached out to touch his clothing (Mark 5:34). He was honoring her trust in coming to him, not setting a rule that anyone who stays sick lacks faith. Elsewhere he healed a man at Bethesda who didn’t even know who Jesus was (John 5:13). Faith brings us to Jesus; the outcome stays in his hands.
