Doubting God? Christian Advice for Seasons of Doubt

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Doubting God is not the same as rejecting him, and honest doubt is not a sin in itself. Scripture is full of believers who questioned God, from the psalmists to John the Baptist and Thomas, and Jesus met them with patience and evidence. What matters most is where you take your doubt: toward God with your questions, or away from him.

Is it a sin to doubt God?

Honest doubt is not a sin. The Bible does draw a line worth understanding, though. Doubt that keeps seeking God is different from unbelief that turns away from him.

Doubt that keeps coming to Jesus

When Peter walked on the water and began to sink, Jesus caught him first and asked his question second:

And immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand, and caught him, and said unto him, O thou of little faith, wherefore didst thou doubt?

Matthew 14:31

Jesus did name Peter’s little faith. But notice the order. The hand came before the question, and Peter was never at risk of being dropped for doubting. After the resurrection, Matthew records that when the disciples saw Jesus, “they worshipped him: but some doubted” (Matthew 28:17). Jesus’ next words were the Great Commission, spoken to the whole group. Doubters were in the room when he sent his church into the world.

Unbelief that walks away

Scripture’s warning is aimed at something else. Hebrews cautions against “an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God” (Hebrews 3:12). The danger there is a heart that has decided to stop listening and leave. Even then, the door stays open: in Jesus’ parable, the father saw his prodigal son “when he was yet a great way off” and ran to him (Luke 15:20). If you’re afraid your questions have already put you out of God’s reach, the fact that you care enough to worry is a sign you’re still seeking.

What does the Bible say about doubt?

These passages show how God responded to people who struggled to believe.

  1. Mark 9:24 — “Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.” A desperate father admitted he had faith and unbelief at the same time. Jesus didn’t tell him to come back when he was sure. He healed the man’s son that day.
  2. Matthew 11:3 — “Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?” John the Baptist sent this question from prison. Jesus answered with evidence: go and tell John what you see and hear. Then he praised John to the crowd (Matthew 11:7-11).
  3. John 20:27 — “Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands; and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side: and be not faithless, but believing.” Thomas said he wouldn’t believe without proof. A week later Jesus came back and offered him exactly that, while calling him onward from doubt to belief.

The Psalms give you words for doubt too. Psalm 13 and Psalm 77 ask whether God has forgotten, and both keep talking to him until they arrive at remembrance and trust. Doubt spoken to God is still prayer.

What should I do when I’m doubting my faith?

  1. Name the doubt precisely. Are you doubting that God exists, that he is good, that he cares about you, or that the Bible is reliable? Each is a different question with different answers. Write yours down.
  2. Bring the question to God himself. Pray it as it is. You don’t have to resolve a doubt before you’re allowed to pray about it.
  3. Look for honest answers. Christians have wrestled carefully with most hard questions for centuries. Ask a pastor to recommend a good book on your specific question, and read the Gospels themselves. John says plainly that he wrote so that readers might believe (John 20:31).
  4. Keep showing up. Keep praying, reading, and gathering with other believers, even when it feels hollow. When many followers walked away, Jesus asked the Twelve if they would leave too, and Peter answered by asking where else they could go (John 6:67-68). Staying is a form of trust.
  5. Tell someone. Doubt grows in isolation. A wise pastor or friend won’t hand you slogans; they’ll sit with the question and walk with you. If your doubt comes with hopelessness or thoughts of not wanting to live, please talk to a doctor or counselor as well, and in the US call or text 988. This prayer for depression and hopelessness may also help.

What can I pray when I don’t know what I believe?

You don’t need to tidy up your uncertainty before you come to God. Pray something like this, and change any words that don’t fit:

Lord, I’m not sure what I believe right now. Part of me wants to trust you, and part of me can’t see you at all. I bring you both. Help my unbelief. Show me what is true, and give me the courage to keep looking for you when it feels like no one is there. I’m staying in this conversation with you, questions and all. Amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal for Christians to have doubts?

Yes. Doubt appears across Scripture and throughout church history, among ordinary believers and respected leaders alike. Seasons of doubt often follow a loss, an unanswered prayer, disappointment with a church, or new questions from study. Having doubts doesn’t mean your faith was never real. It usually means you’re facing hard questions honestly instead of ignoring them.

Does doubting mean I’m not saved?

Doubt doesn’t cancel what Christ has done. Christians across traditions teach that salvation rests on God’s grace, not on how certain you feel on a given day. Many faithful believers have walked through long, dark stretches of doubt and come out still trusting him. If this fear is weighing on you, talk it through with a pastor or priest who can speak to your situation.

What does James 1:6 mean about doubting?

James tells people who ask God for wisdom to ask in faith, “nothing wavering,” and compares a waverer to a wave tossed by the wind. In context, he’s describing a “double minded” person who is divided in loyalty (James 1:8), not someone with honest questions. The verse just before promises that God gives wisdom liberally and without scolding, so bring your questions to him.

Can doubt make your faith stronger?

It can. Faith that has faced hard questions and found God faithful often holds up better than faith that was never tested. That isn’t automatic, because doubt handled alone can harden into cynicism. The difference is usually whether you keep praying, reading, and talking with other believers while you wrestle. Many Christians look back on a season of doubt as a turning point.

Should I keep going to church while I’m doubting?

Usually, yes. Church is meant for people who are still working things out, and hearing others pray and sing can carry you when your own faith feels thin. You don’t have to pretend. Consider telling a pastor or small group leader what you’re wrestling with. If your doubts come from being hurt by a church, it’s reasonable to look for a healthier congregation.

What if I’m doubting because God didn’t answer my prayer?

That is one of the most common and painful roots of doubt, and Scripture includes people who felt it. The prophet Habakkuk asked how long he would cry out while God seemed not to hear (Habakkuk 1:2). Tell God honestly how disappointed you are. An unanswered prayer may mean no, not yet, or something you can’t see now. It doesn’t mean God stopped caring.
