A Prayer for Family and Marriage

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Pray for your family and marriage by asking God to build your home, heal what hurts, and make each of you humble, patient, and quick to forgive. Bring each person to God by name, start with your own part, and pair prayer with steps like apology, honest conversation, and counseling. No verse requires staying in an abusive home.

Care note: If there is abuse, threats, or coercive control in your home, your safety and your children’s safety come first. No verse about marriage, submission, or forgiveness requires you to stay in danger. Contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233 in the US, or chat on its website) or local help, and call 911 if you are in immediate danger. For serious conflict, a pastor or licensed marriage counselor can help. If you are thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US), or contact local emergency services.

What Bible verses can I pray for my family and marriage?

  • Psalm 127:1 — “Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.” The builders still build and the watchman still watches. The psalm doesn’t tell you to stop working on your home, only that the work depends on God. Pray it plainly: Lord, build this house.
  • Ephesians 4:2–3 — “With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love; Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.” Here “lowliness” is humility, “longsuffering” is patience, and “forbearing” means bearing with each other. “Endeavouring” tells you unity takes effort. It doesn’t just happen.
  • Ecclesiastes 4:12 — “And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.” Read with the verses before it, Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 is about friendship and partnership in general, though it’s often read at weddings. Many couples picture God as the third strand. The plainer point is still worth praying: we’re stronger together than alone.
  • Joshua 24:15 — “as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.” Joshua said this while calling all Israel to “choose you this day whom ye will serve.” He could only answer for his own household. You can decide how you will lead and love at home, even when others aren’t there yet.

How do I pray for my marriage?

  1. Start with yourself. Before asking God to change your spouse, ask him to show you your part. Jesus warned about seeing the speck in someone else’s eye and missing the beam in our own (Matthew 7:3–5).
  2. Pray for your spouse by name. Pray for their good, not only their change: their health, work, faith, rest, and the burdens they carry that you might not see.
  3. Name the real tension. Money, in-laws, intimacy, screens, grief, a hard season with the kids. Specific prayers keep you honest.
  4. Pray together, briefly, if you both want to. One minute is enough. Hold hands, pray the Lord’s Prayer, or each say one sentence. Never use prayer to lecture your spouse about their faults.
  5. Act on what you prayed. Apologize, plan the hard conversation, set aside time together, or call a counselor. Asking for outside help is wisdom, not failure. For conflict and communication, see Christian advice for relationships.

How do I keep praying for a struggling family?

Some family prayers go unanswered for a long time: an adult child who has walked away, siblings who don’t speak, a parent whose illness is changing everyone’s life. Jesus knew we would be tempted to quit.

And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint;

Luke 18:1

To “faint” here means to lose heart. The parable that follows is about a widow who kept asking a judge for justice until he answered. You may pray for someone for years. Keep praying, keep the door open where it’s safe, and keep loving in the small ways available to you. You can’t choose for grown children or relatives, but you can bring them to God every day. If an old family wound is still raw, the daily devotional on forgiveness is a good place to start.

What if my spouse won’t pray with me?

Pray alone, and keep praying for your spouse. Jesus often prayed by himself (Luke 6:12). Don’t pressure your husband or wife or treat prayer as a test of the marriage, which tends to push people further away.

Ask gently what makes praying together hard. For some, praying aloud feels awkward or exposed. You might try a moment of silent prayer together, reading a short prayer, or simply asking, Can I pray for you about that? Faithful, quiet prayer for your spouse is never wasted.

Does the Bible say I have to stay in an abusive marriage?

No. Scripture’s instructions to husbands and wives begin with “Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God” (Ephesians 5:21), and they call husbands to love “even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it” (Ephesians 5:25). Headship in the New Testament is described as self-giving love. It never gives permission to control, threaten, or harm. Catholic and Protestant churches teach differently about divorce and remarriage, but they widely recognize that a spouse may separate to escape danger. Get safe first, then seek wise help.

Lord, unless you build this house, we labor in vain. Build our home. I bring you [spouse’s name] and [the names of your children or family members]. You know what is heavy here today: [name it].

Before I ask you to change anyone else, show me my part. Where I have been harsh, proud, distant, or unfair, forgive me, and give me the humility to say so.

Give us patience with one another and grace to forgive quickly. Teach us to listen, and guard our words. Where trust is broken, rebuild it honestly and in your time.

Protect our children, draw back to you anyone who has wandered, and keep every one of us safe. Hold us together with your love. As for me and my house, we will serve you. In Jesus’ name, amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can prayer save a marriage?

God can change hearts, and prayer invites him into a marriage in a way nothing else can. But prayer isn’t a formula, and it doesn’t override another person’s choices. It usually works alongside repentance, honest conversation, counseling, and time. Keep praying whatever happens. God stays with you whether the marriage is restored or you are grieving a loss you didn’t choose.

What is a short prayer for my family?

Try this: Lord, bless and keep my family today. Give us patience with one another, protect us from harm, and draw each of us closer to you. Help me love them well. Amen. Short prayers like this are easy to pray at breakfast, in the car, or at bedtime. Add each person’s name and one specific need to make it your own.

How do I pray for my children?

Pray for each child by name: for their faith, safety, friendships, character, and the choices ahead of them. Many parents pray the priestly blessing from Numbers 6:24–26 over their children at bedtime. Pray about what worries you, and also thank God for what you see growing in them. Let older children know you pray for them, without using prayer to pressure them.

Should I pray for a spouse who has hurt me?

You can, and Jesus calls us to pray even for those who mistreat us. Pray for their repentance, honesty, and healing, and pray for wisdom for yourself. Praying for someone isn’t excusing what they did, and it doesn’t mean staying where you are unsafe. If you are afraid of your spouse, pray from a safe place and reach out for help.

What Bible verses help a struggling marriage?

Many couples return to 1 Corinthians 13:4–7, which describes love that is patient and doesn’t keep a ledger of wrongs. Colossians 3:13–14 asks us to forgive as Christ forgave and to “put on charity,” meaning love. Ephesians 4:32 calls for tenderhearted forgiveness. Read one slowly each day and turn it into a prayer for your spouse and yourself.
