Bible Verses and a Prayer for Temptation

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

The Bible says temptation comes to everyone, and that God is faithful: he "will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape" (1 Corinthians 10:13). Being tempted is not the same as sinning. Jesus was tempted too, and he gives help to anyone who asks.

Care note: If a temptation has become a compulsion or an addiction, you don't have to fight it alone. Prayer and Scripture support recovery, but they don't replace a pastor, a recovery group, or a doctor. In the US, the SAMHSA National Helpline is 1-800-662-4357. If you are thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) or contact local emergency services.

What does the Bible say about temptation?

Scripture never pretends Christians stop feeling pulled toward sin, and it never leaves them without help. Start with the promise most believers lean on first.

There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.

1 Corinthians 10:13

"Suffer" here means allow. Paul wrote to a church surrounded by idol feasts and sexual immorality, and in the very next verse he tells them to flee from idolatry. The way of escape is usually something you walk through on purpose. It rarely just happens to you.

  • Hebrews 4:15–16 — "For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need." Jesus knows the pull from the inside, so you can come to him boldly in the moment itself.
  • Matthew 26:41 — "Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak." Jesus said this in Gethsemane to disciples who kept falling asleep. Prayer before the hard moment matters as much as prayer during it.
  • James 1:14–15 — "But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death." In the KJV, "lust" means desire of any kind. James traces a path from desire to choice to consequence, so there are early places to stop.
  • James 4:7–8 — "Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you." Resisting begins with turning toward God. Willpower runs out; nearness to God is where lasting strength comes from.
  • 1 John 1:9 — "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." When you have given in, this is the way back.

Is it a sin to be tempted?

No. Temptation is the invitation, and sin is saying yes to it. Hebrews says Jesus was tempted in every way we are, yet without sin, so the feeling of being pulled can't itself be sin. James 1:13 adds that God never tempts anyone to do evil. Temptation comes from our own desires, from a broken world, and, Scripture says, from the devil.

The difference matters because shame often says you're already guilty the moment a thought appears, so you might as well give in. That lie makes temptation stronger. You can bring the temptation itself to God, before anything has happened, and ask for help without hiding.

How did Jesus resist temptation?

Matthew 4 describes Jesus in the wilderness after forty days of fasting. The devil aimed at real hunger, at a desire for proof, and at a shortcut to power. Each time, Jesus answered with Scripture from Deuteronomy. To the first test he said, "It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (Matthew 4:4).

He didn't debate the tempter. He answered with specific words he already knew, which is what Psalm 119:11 means by hiding God's word in the heart. Memorizing one or two verses for your particular struggle gives you something true to say when your own thoughts get loud.

How do I pray when I'm tempted right now?

A prayer in the moment can be short. These steps take a minute or two:

  1. Name it. Tell God plainly what you're being pulled toward. Keeping it vague tends to give it more power.
  2. Say a verse out loud. Pick one from this page and speak it, the way Jesus answered with Scripture.
  3. Ask for the way out. 1 Corinthians 10:13 says one exists. Ask God to show you the next step.
  4. Take that step. Leave the room, put your phone somewhere else, go for a walk, or text someone you trust.

Lord Jesus, you know what it is to be tempted, and you know what I'm facing right now. I don't want to give in. Thank you that you are faithful and that you have promised a way out.

Show it to me, and give me the courage to take it. Keep me close to you. And when I fail, help me come back to you quickly instead of hiding. Amen.

What if I keep giving in to the same temptation?

Repeated failure is discouraging, but it isn't proof that God has given up on you. Confess it honestly (1 John 1:9) and receive forgiveness without trying to earn it. Then look at the pattern. When does it happen? What are you usually feeling beforehand: tired, lonely, angry, bored? Changing what you can around those moments is part of watching and praying.

Don't carry it alone. Galatians 6:1–2 pictures believers gently restoring one another and bearing each other's burdens. A trusted friend, pastor, or small group who will ask how you're doing helps. If you need words for the moment after a fall, this confession prayer after failure may help, and if the struggle has become an addiction, see this prayer for addiction recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "lead us not into temptation" mean?

In the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:13), Jesus teaches his followers to ask God to keep them from trials they can't withstand and to deliver them from evil. It doesn't mean God lures people into sin; James 1:13 says he tempts no one. It is a humble prayer from people who know they need God's protection.

Why does God allow temptation?

Scripture doesn't give a complete explanation, but it shows God using testing to strengthen faith. James 1:12 calls the person who endures temptation blessed, and Jesus himself faced temptation before his public ministry began. God never causes anyone to sin, yet he can use the struggle to teach us dependence on him.

What is the "way to escape" in 1 Corinthians 10:13?

Paul doesn't name a single method. The way out might be leaving a place, ending a conversation, refusing the first step, calling a friend, or holding steady without giving in, since the verse says God makes you "able to bear it." The next verse tells the Corinthians to flee idolatry, which suggests escape usually means moving away rather than testing how close you can get.

Can the devil make me sin?

The Bible presents the devil as a real tempter, but not as someone who can force your choices. James 4:7 says to resist him and he will flee, and James 1:14 locates temptation's pull in our own desires. You are responsible for what you choose, and you are also not alone. God gives grace to resist and forgiveness when you fail.

Should I tell someone about my temptation?

Often, yes. James 5:16 encourages believers to confess their faults to one another and pray for one another. Choose someone mature and trustworthy, such as a pastor, mentor, or close Christian friend, who will keep your confidence. Speaking a struggle out loud usually loosens its grip, and it lets someone pray for you and check in.
