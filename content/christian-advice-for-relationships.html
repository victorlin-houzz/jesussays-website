Christian Advice for Relationships

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

The Bible’s advice for relationships comes down to a few steady habits: listen before you speak, answer gently, take conflict to the person directly, forgive, and choose people who draw you toward God. Healthy boundaries are biblical too. Christian love is patient and humble, but it never asks you to accept abuse, threats, or control.

Care note: If a partner, date, or family member hurts, threatens, controls, or isolates you, that isn’t a communication problem to solve with better listening, and your safety comes first. Contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 (US) or local help, and call 911 if you are in immediate danger. A pastor or licensed counselor can help with hard relationships. If you are thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US), or contact local emergency services.

What does the Bible say about healthy relationships?

Much of Scripture’s relationship wisdom is about the tongue. These verses apply to dating, marriage, friendship, and family alike.

  • James 1:19 — “Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.” Many arguments escalate because both people are talking and neither feels heard. Before you answer, try repeating back what you heard and ask whether you got it right.
  • Proverbs 15:1 — “A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.” A lower voice and a gentler word often end a fight faster than a winning point.
  • Ephesians 4:29 — “Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.” “Corrupt” means rotten, and “edifying” means building up. Before you speak, ask whether your words will build this person up or tear them down.

What does the Bible say about Christian dating?

The Bible doesn’t describe modern dating, but it speaks clearly about the partnership dating can lead to.

Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?

2 Corinthians 6:14

A yoke joined two animals pulling a plow together. Paul was warning the Corinthian church against partnerships that would pull them back toward idol worship, and Christians have long applied the verse to marriage. Since dating is often how people choose a spouse, it matters here too. It’s a question of shared direction, not a judgment on anyone’s worth.

A few honest questions help:

  • Does this relationship draw both of you toward God, or pull one of you away?
  • Does the other person respect your no the first time, about time, money, church, or physical limits?
  • Can you be fully honest, or are you starting to hide things from friends and family?
  • Do people who love you and know you well see good fruit in it?

Historic Christian teaching, Catholic and Protestant, holds that sex belongs within marriage, so decide on physical boundaries together before you are in the moment. Watch for warning signs as well: jealousy presented as love, pressure, checking your phone, cutting you off from friends, or anger that frightens you.

How should Christians handle conflict in a relationship?

  1. Go to the person first. Jesus said, “if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone” (Matthew 18:15). Talk to them before you talk about them, whether to friends, family, or social media.
  2. Listen until they feel heard. Apply James 1:19 before you defend yourself.
  3. Keep to one issue. Describe what happened and how it affected you, without reaching for always and never.
  4. Apologize specifically. Name what you did, without adding but. Then ask what would help make it right.
  5. Don’t let it fester. Paul says not to let the sun go down on your wrath (Ephesians 4:26). If you need to pause, say so and set a time to come back to it.
  6. Get help if you’re stuck. A pastor, mentor, or counselor can see what two frustrated people can’t.

If the conflict has left a wound you’re struggling to release, the daily devotional on forgiveness may help.

Are boundaries biblical?

Yes. Scripture tells you to guard what is inside you:

Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.

Proverbs 4:23

Here “issues” means the springs or outflow of a life. Keeping your heart doesn’t mean building walls. It means taking care of the place your love comes from. Jesus himself often withdrew from the crowds to pray (Luke 5:16), and he didn’t entrust himself to everyone (John 2:24). Proverbs also recognizes that some relationships pull us toward harm:

Make no friendship with an angry man; and with a furious man thou shalt not go: Lest thou learn his ways, and get a snare to thy soul.

Proverbs 22:24–25

A boundary might be saying no, limiting contact, leaving a conversation that turns cruel, or ending a relationship. Some relationships carry wounds that prayer alone won’t mend right away: betrayal, estrangement, years of conflict. You can bring them to God without having an answer yet. Reconciliation isn’t always possible, and sometimes it isn’t safe. If you are married or praying for family, see the prayer for family and marriage.

How can I pray over a relationship with Scripture?

This takes about ten minutes. Choose one relationship, not all of them. Read one of the verses above slowly, twice. Write one honest sentence about how you actually feel. Pray using the verse’s words. Then name one small next step: an apology, a phone call, better listening in your next conversation, or a boundary you need to keep.

Lord, I bring you my relationship with [name]. You know what has been said and what hasn’t. Make me swift to hear and slow to speak. Give me words that build up instead of tearing down. Where I have caused harm, give me the humility to own it. Where I have been hurt, help me forgive, even slowly. Give me wisdom to know where to draw near and where to draw a line. Let your love, not my effort alone, be the ground we stand on. Amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it wrong for a Christian to date a non-Christian?

Many pastors counsel against it, because dating often leads to marriage and faith shapes nearly everything in a shared life: priorities, money, Sundays, and how children are raised. It isn’t a verdict on the other person’s character. If you are already in that situation, talk honestly with your pastor and with the person you’re dating about what your faith means to you.

How do I know if my relationship is unhealthy?

Pay attention to fear. If you watch every word to avoid an explosion, if you are cut off from friends or family, if your phone or money is controlled, or if you are pressured after saying no, the relationship is unhealthy and may be abusive. Talk to someone you trust, and contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 (US) for confidential help.

How do I repair a broken relationship?

Start with your own part. Apologize specifically, without excuses, and ask what the other person needs from you. Then give it time, because trust returns through consistent behavior, not a single conversation. Some relationships heal and some don’t, and that doesn’t depend on you alone. If the other person isn’t willing yet, you can still forgive, pray for them, and leave the door open where it’s safe.

What does the Bible say about loving difficult people?

Jesus told his followers to love their enemies and do good to those who hate them (Luke 6:27). In Scripture, love is something you do: praying for someone, speaking fairly about them, refusing revenge. It doesn’t require closeness with someone who keeps hurting you. You can love a difficult person well while keeping clear limits on how much access they have to your life.

Is it biblical to set boundaries with parents?

Honoring your father and mother is a lifelong command (Exodus 20:12), but honor isn’t the same as unlimited access or, for adults, obedience in every decision. Genesis 2:24 describes leaving father and mother when you marry. You can speak respectfully, help parents in need, and still limit contact that harms you, your marriage, or your children.
