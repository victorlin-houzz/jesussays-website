Grief and Loss: Questions Christians Ask

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

It is not wrong for a Christian to grieve. Jesus wept at his friend Lazarus's tomb (John 11:35), and Paul tells believers they will sorrow, only "not, even as others which have no hope" (1 Thessalonians 4:13). Grief is love continuing after a loss. Scripture doesn't rush it, and it promises that God stays near the brokenhearted while they mourn.

Care note: Scripture and prayer can comfort you in grief, but they don't replace a pastor, a grief counselor, a support group, or your doctor. If grief has left you feeling hopeless or thinking about ending your life, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) now. Outside the US, contact local emergency services.

Is it wrong for a Christian to grieve?

But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

1 Thessalonians 4:13–14

Paul doesn't tell grieving Christians to stop sorrowing. He tells them their sorrow has a different shape because Jesus rose from the dead. Hope and tears can sit in the same hour. At Lazarus's tomb, Jesus knew he was about to raise his friend, and still "Jesus wept" (John 11:35). He didn't hurry Mary and Martha past their grief. He entered it with them.

Much of the Bible's prayer book is lament. Psalms of grief, protest, and longing are there so God's people have words when their own run out. Crying, numbness, anger, exhaustion, and trouble concentrating are all part of how grief can feel. None of them means your faith has failed.

What does the Bible say to comfort those who grieve?

  • Psalm 34:18 — "The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit." "Nigh" means near, and "contrite" carries the sense of crushed. You don't have to feel composed for God to be close.
  • Matthew 5:4 — "Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted." Jesus places mourners among the blessed. Comfort is promised to them, even when it comes slowly.
  • Psalm 23:4 — "Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me." The psalm doesn't skip the valley. It promises company in it.
  • Revelation 21:4 — "And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away." This is where the story is headed. It doesn't make today's loss small. It tells you death won't have the last word.

Why did God let this happen?

This is often the hardest question, and the Bible doesn't answer it with a formula. Job never learned why he suffered, but God met him. Be wary of quick explanations, including ones offered by well-meaning people. Romans 8:28 promises that God works all things together for good for those who love him. It does not say the loss itself is good, or that you should feel grateful for it.

What Scripture does show is that God doesn't watch suffering from a distance. In Jesus he entered it: he grieved, died, and rose again. You may live with unanswered questions for a long time. You can keep bringing them to God as questions, and he is patient with them.

Is it okay to be angry at God after a loss?

Believers in Scripture took their anger and confusion straight to God. David prayed, "How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?" (Psalm 13:1). Job argued his case, and Lamentations pours out raw complaint over a ruined city. These prayers are in the Bible on purpose. Psalm 13 ends in trust, but only after that honest beginning. Bringing your anger to God is a way of staying in the relationship instead of walking away from it.

How do I pray when I'm grieving?

Grief can make prayer feel impossible. Short, honest prayers are enough. Say the person's name. Tell God what you miss and what you're afraid of. Ask for one thing: sleep tonight, strength for the funeral, someone to sit with you. Some days you may only manage to sit quietly, and that counts too. If a guided quiet minute helps, try Still Waters. If loneliness has settled in since the loss, these Bible verses for loneliness may help.

Lord, I miss them. This hurts more than I can say. You wept at your friend's grave, so I know you understand these tears. Stay close to me when I can't feel you near. Carry me through today and give me what I need for tonight. Hold my questions until I can bring them to you again. Comfort me, and don't let me grieve without hope. Amen.

How can I help someone who is grieving?

When Job's friends first arrived, they sat on the ground with him for seven days and said nothing, because they saw how great his grief was (Job 2:13). That was the best thing they did. When they later started explaining his suffering, they made it worse. Presence matters more than explanations.

Romans 12:15 asks us to weep with those who weep. In practice, that means saying the person's name, bringing a meal, handling a chore, and checking in weeks after the funeral, when the calls tend to stop. Skip phrases like "God needed another angel" or "everything happens for a reason." A simple "I'm so sorry, and I'm here" is usually better.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I see my loved one again in heaven?

The Christian hope is resurrection. Paul writes that God will bring with Jesus those who have died in him (1 Thessalonians 4:14), and Jesus told Martha that he is the resurrection and the life (John 11:25). Only God knows each person's heart, so if you're unsure about someone you've lost, you can entrust them to his mercy and justice, which are greater and wiser than ours.

How long does grief last?

The Bible sets no timetable for grief. Israel mourned Moses for thirty days (Deuteronomy 34:8), but that was a custom, not a limit on sorrow. Grief often softens and changes shape over time without disappearing, and anniversaries can bring it back. If grief isn't easing after many months or is making daily life very hard, talk with a doctor or grief counselor.

Can I grieve losses that aren't a death?

Yes. People grieve miscarriage, divorce, a diagnosis, a lost job, an ended friendship, or a future they had hoped for. The Bible's laments cover exile, betrayal, illness, and ruin as well as death. Calling a loss a loss and bringing it to God is honest. It isn't ingratitude for what you still have.

Why does God feel far away when I'm grieving?

Grief can numb every feeling, including your sense of God's presence. The psalmists described the same thing and asked why God seemed hidden. Feeling far from God doesn't mean he has left. Keep praying the simple prayers you can manage, let others pray for you when you can't, and stay connected to your church, even if you sit quietly in the back for a while.

Is it wrong to feel relief after someone dies?

No. After a long illness or a painful season of caregiving, relief that the suffering is over is common and often sits right beside deep sorrow. It doesn't mean you loved the person less. Mixed feelings, including guilt or regret, can all be brought to God honestly. He knows the whole story, and he can hold every part of it with you.
