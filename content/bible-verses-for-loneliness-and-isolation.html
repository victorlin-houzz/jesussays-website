Bible Verses for Loneliness and Isolation

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Bible verses for loneliness include Psalm 139:7-10, Deuteronomy 31:8, Psalm 142:4-5, Isaiah 49:15-16, Psalm 68:6, and John 16:32. Scripture treats loneliness as real; God himself said “It is not good that the man should be alone.” The Bible answers it in two ways: with God’s constant presence, and with his habit of placing lonely people among others.

Care note: Long isolation is hard, and it can slide into something heavier. If loneliness has turned into despair, or you have thoughts of not wanting to live, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the US, or contact local emergency services if you live elsewhere. A pastor, doctor, or counselor can also help you find next steps.

Which Bible verses speak to loneliness?

Quoted from the King James Version, with a note on each one’s setting.

  1. Psalm 139:7-10 — “Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there. If I take the wings of the morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea; Even there shall thy hand lead me, and thy right hand shall hold me.” “Hell” here translates Sheol, the grave or realm of the dead. David can’t find a place, however far or low, where God is absent.
  2. Deuteronomy 31:8 — “And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee: fear not, neither be dismayed.” Moses said this to Joshua as Moses was about to die and leave him. If you have lost the person you leaned on most, this promise was first spoken into that kind of loss. Hebrews 13:5 repeats it for Christians.
  3. Psalm 142:4-5 — “I looked on my right hand, and beheld, but there was no man that would know me: refuge failed me; no man cared for my soul. I cried unto thee, O LORD: I said, Thou art my refuge and my portion in the land of the living.” David prayed this while hiding in a cave. “No man cared for my soul” is one of the most honest lines in Scripture, and he said it to God.
  4. Isaiah 49:15-16 — “Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands.” This answers people who were saying, “my Lord hath forgotten me” (Isaiah 49:14). Even if the people closest to you forget, God does not.
  5. Psalm 68:6 — “God setteth the solitary in families.” God’s answer to loneliness often comes through people. This isn’t a promise of marriage or a particular household. In the New Testament, the church becomes family for people who don’t have one.
  6. John 16:32 — “Behold, the hour cometh, yea, is now come, that ye shall be scattered, every man to his own, and shall leave me alone: and yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me.” Jesus knew his closest friends were about to abandon him. He named it plainly and held on to his Father’s presence.

Is it wrong to feel lonely if God is with me?

No. Before sin entered the world, in a good garden with God present, God looked at Adam alone and said it was not good (Genesis 2:18). Loneliness isn’t proof that your faith is weak. It reflects how God made you: for himself and for other people.

Paul, near the end of his life, felt both at once:

At my first answer no man stood with me, but all men forsook me: I pray God that it may not be laid to their charge. Notwithstanding the Lord stood with me, and strengthened me.

2 Timothy 4:16-17

In the same letter he asked Timothy to “come shortly” (2 Timothy 4:9). Paul knew the Lord stood with him, and he still wanted his friend. You can hold both of those too.

What does the Bible say to do when you feel isolated?

When Elijah ran for his life, he told God, “I, even I only, am left” (1 Kings 19:10). God gently corrected him: seven thousand others in Israel had stayed faithful (1 Kings 19:18). Then God gave him a companion, Elisha. Loneliness can tell you a story that isn’t the whole truth, such as “no one cares” or “no one wants to hear from me.” Scripture’s pattern is to test that story and take a small step toward people.

  • Reach out once this week. One text, one call, one coffee invitation. Keep it small.
  • Show up somewhere regularly. Hebrews 10:25 urges believers not to give up meeting together. A worship service, small group, Bible study, or volunteer shift helps belonging grow through repetition.
  • Serve someone. Visiting a homebound neighbor or helping at church puts you beside people without the pressure of small talk.
  • Tell a pastor. Many churches can connect you with a group or a visitor, especially if you are homebound, widowed, or new in town.
  • Get support if loneliness is tangled with depression or anxiety. A counselor can help with what friendship alone may not reach. These Bible verses for depression may also help.

If church has been a place of hurt for you, it’s okay to go slowly and start with one trusted person.

How do I pray when I feel completely alone?

Psalm 142 gives a pattern: pour out the complaint, name the loneliness plainly, then turn to God as refuge. You can pray the same way:

Lord, I feel alone, and I’m tired of it. Some days it seems like no one notices me at all. You say you see me and have not forgotten me, so I’m telling you the truth. Be my refuge today. Make your presence known to me in a way I can recognize. Bring one real connection into my life this week, and give me courage to reach back. Show me someone else who is lonely, too. Amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did Jesus ever feel lonely?

Yes. In Gethsemane his closest friends fell asleep while he prayed, and he asked Peter, “What, could ye not watch with me one hour?” (Matthew 26:40). On the cross he cried out, “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” Hebrews says he can be “touched with the feeling of our infirmities” (Hebrews 4:15). He knows loneliness from the inside.

Which psalm is best for loneliness?

Psalm 139 is for feeling unseen, since it describes being fully known and never out of God’s reach. Psalm 142 is for when no one seems to care. Psalm 25:16 is a one-line prayer: “Turn thee unto me, and have mercy upon me; for I am desolate and afflicted.” In Psalm 102 the writer feels like “a sparrow alone upon the house top.”

What does the Bible say about being single and lonely?

Jesus was single, and Paul lived unmarried and called it a gift for some (1 Corinthians 7:7-8). Singleness isn’t a lesser life, and marriage doesn’t cure loneliness. Jesus promised that those who give things up for him would receive “brethren, and sisters” among his people (Mark 10:29-30). Loneliness in singleness is real, so bring it honestly to God and let the church be family.

Can you feel lonely in a marriage or at church?

Yes. Loneliness is about connection, not the number of people around you. You can sit in a full sanctuary or share a home and still feel unknown. Say so gently to your spouse or a trusted friend, and consider talking with a pastor or counselor. If you feel unsafe at home, contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 in the US, or local help elsewhere.

How can I help someone who is lonely?

Be specific and consistent. Invite them to a particular thing at a particular time, then invite them again. Sit with them at church. Call the widow, the new neighbor, or the friend who stopped showing up. Paul told believers to “weep with them that weep” (Romans 12:15). Small, repeated contact usually means more than one big gesture, and it is often how God sets the solitary in families.
