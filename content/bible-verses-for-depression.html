Bible Verses for Depression

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Bible verses that speak to depression include Psalm 34:18, Psalm 42:11, Psalm 40:1-2, Isaiah 42:3, Psalm 139:11-12, and Romans 8:38-39. None of them promise a quick fix or tell you to cheer up. They give honest words to despair and show a God who stays near the brokenhearted, even when you can’t feel him there.

Care note: These verses are meant to sit alongside real help, not replace it. If depression is affecting your sleep, work, relationships, or will to live, please talk with a doctor, licensed counselor, or pastor. In a crisis in the US, call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. If you live elsewhere, call your local emergency number.

What are the best Bible verses for depression?

Read these one at a time rather than all at once. Each is quoted from the King James Version, with a short note on what it means in its setting.

  1. Psalm 34:18 — “The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.” “Nigh” means near, and “contrite” here carries the sense of crushed. God moves toward crushed people, not away from them.
  2. Psalm 42:11 — “Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.” The psalmist talks back to his own soul instead of pretending he’s fine. The same refrain appears three times across Psalms 42 and 43, as if he had to keep saying it. So may you.
  3. Psalm 40:1-2 — “I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.” David describes a pit with no footing and a rescue that came after waiting. It’s a testimony, not a timetable.
  4. Isaiah 42:3 — “A bruised reed shall he not break, and the smoking flax shall he not quench.” Matthew applies this to Jesus (Matthew 12:20). Smoking flax is a lamp wick that has almost gone out. If that describes your faith right now, he handles it gently.
  5. Psalm 139:11-12 — “If I say, Surely the darkness shall cover me; even the night shall be light about me. Yea, the darkness hideth not from thee; but the night shineth as the day: the darkness and the light are both alike to thee.” Depression can feel like a fog that hides you from everyone, God included. This psalm says it can’t hide you from him.
  6. Romans 8:38-39 — “For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.” Paul includes “things present.” Whatever you are living through today is on that list.

Is depression a sin or a sign of weak faith?

No. Scripture is full of faithful people who sank low. Elijah asked to die (1 Kings 19:4). Jeremiah cursed the day he was born. Paul wrote that he and his companions “despaired even of life” (2 Corinthians 1:8). In Gethsemane, Jesus told his friends his soul was sorrowful to the point of death. None of them are presented as failures of faith for feeling that way.

Depression can involve the body, the mind, hard circumstances, and spiritual struggle all at once. It rarely has one simple cause. Job’s friends assumed his suffering must be his own fault, and at the end God told them they had not spoken of him “the thing that is right” (Job 42:7). Be wary of anyone, including the voice in your own head, who turns your pain into a verdict on your faith. Seeing a doctor or counselor is wisdom and good care of the life God gave you.

Why is Psalm 88 in the Bible?

Psalm 88 may be the darkest prayer in Scripture. Its writer, Heman, says “my life draweth nigh unto the grave,” and the psalm closes without any turn toward relief: “Lover and friend hast thou put far from me, and mine acquaintance into darkness” (Psalm 88:18).

Its place in the Bible matters. God allowed a prayer with no happy ending to stand in his book. If you can’t honestly end your prayers with praise right now, you are in good company. Look at how the psalm opens, too: “O LORD God of my salvation.” Heman is in the dark, and he is still talking to God. In depression, faith may look exactly like that.

How can I use these verses when reading feels hard?

Depression can make concentration difficult, so start small.

  1. Choose one verse for the week. Write it on a card, set it as your lock screen, or tape it to the mirror. One verse taken in slowly does more than a chapter skimmed.
  2. Listen instead of reading. An audio Bible, or a friend reading a psalm aloud to you, can carry the words when your eyes won’t.
  3. Make it personal. Psalm 34:18 can become a prayer: Lord, you are near me, and my heart is broken.
  4. Pair it with a person. Text the verse to someone you trust and tell them you’re in a hard stretch. Scripture and community work together.
  5. Come back tomorrow. Returning to the same verse isn’t a failure. It’s how words sink in.

When you’re ready to put your own words to God, this prayer for depression and hopelessness may help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did anyone in the Bible have depression?

The Bible doesn’t use modern medical terms, but it describes people in deep and lasting despair. David wrote that he flooded his bed with tears all night (Psalm 6:6). Hannah prayed “in bitterness of soul” (1 Samuel 1:10). Moses and Jonah each asked God to take their lives. God met them with patience, and none of them lost their place among his people.

Can Bible verses cure depression?

Scripture can comfort you, steady your thinking, and remind you that God is near, and many believers treasure verses that carried them through dark seasons. But verses are not a treatment plan, and memorizing more of them is not a test of faith. Depression usually needs more than one kind of help, so pair Scripture and prayer with care from a doctor or licensed counselor.

Which psalm is best for depression?

Different psalms fit different days. Psalms 42 and 43 suit the days you are talking yourself toward hope. Psalm 13 is for asking God how long this will last. Psalm 88 is for when you have no hope left to speak of. Psalm 40 is a testimony from someone lifted out of a pit, and Psalm 23 is for walking through the valley. Read one aloud as your own prayer.

What should I say to a Christian friend who is depressed?

Start with presence more than answers. Job’s friends did best in the first week, when “none spake a word unto him: for they saw that his grief was very great” (Job 2:13). They went wrong once they started explaining. Skip “just pray more” and quick verses. Say “I’m here,” ask what would help, and gently encourage professional care.

Is it normal for depression to make me doubt God?

Yes. Depression can cloud your sense of God along with everything else, and doubt in a dark season is not the same as walking away from him. The writer of Psalm 77 asked, “Hath God forgotten to be gracious?” and that question is part of Scripture. Bring your doubts to God as honestly as that, and talk them through with a pastor or mature believer you trust.
