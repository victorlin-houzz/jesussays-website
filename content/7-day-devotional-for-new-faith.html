A 7-Day Devotional for New Believers

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

This 7-day devotional for new believers gives you one Bible passage, a short reflection, and a one-line prayer or next step for each day, about ten minutes at a time. Across the week it covers what every new Christian needs first: God’s love, grace, a new identity, forgiveness, prayer, reading the Bible, and belonging to a church.

How do I use this 7-day devotional?

Find ten quiet minutes, ideally at the same time each day. Read the passage slowly, read the key verse a second time, then read the reflection and pray or take the step. Writing one sentence a day in a notebook helps. If you miss a day, pick up where you left off. Key verses are from the King James Version; if its older English feels hard going, read the passage in a modern translation too.

The 7-day plan for new believers

Day 1: God loves you

Read: John 3:1-21

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

John 3:16

Nicodemus was a respected teacher, and he still came to Jesus at night with questions. Jesus spoke to him of new birth and of God’s love for the world, and your faith rests on that same love. You didn’t earn God’s love by deciding to follow him; you responded to a love that was already there. Verse 17 adds that Jesus came to save the world, not to condemn it.

Pray: Father, thank you for loving me before I ever turned to you.

Day 2: Saved by grace

Read: Ephesians 2:1-10

For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.

Ephesians 2:8-9

Many new Christians quietly wonder whether they’ve done enough. Paul’s answer is plain: salvation is a gift, received through faith. Verse 10 goes on to call us God’s workmanship, created for good works. Good works grow out of grace; they don’t buy it. On a bad day you are no less loved than on a good one.

Step: Write verse 8 somewhere you’ll see it, and read it whenever you feel you have to earn your place.

Day 3: You are a new creation

Read: 2 Corinthians 5:14-21

Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.

2 Corinthians 5:17

“New creature” means something has begun in you that wasn’t there before. That doesn’t mean old habits or temptations vanish overnight, and many new believers are surprised by how much stays the same at first. The change is real, and it keeps growing as you walk with Christ.

Pray: Lord, make me new in the places I can’t change on my own.

Day 4: Forgiven, and forgiven again

Read: 1 John 1:5-2:2

If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

1 John 1:9

You will still sin as a Christian. John wrote this letter to believers, and he assumes they’ll need to confess. Confession is telling God the truth and receiving the forgiveness Jesus has already won for you. Some Christians confess privately to God, and some also confess to a pastor or priest; both rest on this same promise. Our confession prayer guide can help you start.

Step: Tell God one specific thing you regret, then thank him that he is faithful to forgive.

Day 5: Learning to pray

Read: Matthew 6:5-15

But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly.

Matthew 6:6

A “closet” in the King James Version is an inner room. The point is privacy and honesty: prayer is first a conversation with your Father, with no audience to impress. You don’t need special words. Talk to God plainly, then pray the Lord’s Prayer in verses 9-13 slowly, one line at a time. For more help, see how to pray according to the Bible.

Step: Choose a place and time to pray for five minutes tomorrow.

Day 6: Growing through God’s word

Read: 1 Peter 2:1-10

As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby

1 Peter 2:2

“Sincere” here means pure. Babies don’t feel guilty about being hungry; they simply need to be fed, often. Peter says new believers are meant to want Scripture the same way. The Gospel of John is a good place to start, one chapter a day. As you read, ask two questions: What does this show me about God? What does it ask of me?

Step: Read John chapter 1 today, and plan to read one chapter a day after this week.

Day 7: You belong to a church family

Read: Acts 2:37-47

And they continued stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.

Acts 2:42

The first Christians didn’t follow Jesus alone. Right after they believed and were baptized, they “continued stedfastly,” that is, they kept devoting themselves to four things together: the apostles’ teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread, and prayer. You don’t need to have everything figured out to walk in the door.

Step: Visit a local church this week, introduce yourself to one person, and ask a pastor about baptism and next steps.

What should a new Christian do after the first week?

Keep the rhythm going, even if it’s short. Finish John a chapter a day, then read Mark or Luke. Keep praying, with the Lord’s Prayer as a guide. Stay with one church long enough to know people, and tell one trusted person you’ve decided to follow Jesus. Expect questions and dry days. They’re a normal part of growing, and doubt doesn’t mean your faith isn’t real. If you’re also carrying something heavy, like grief, addiction, or depression, lean on your church and seek counseling or medical care too. Faith and good care work together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Bible translation should a new believer use?

Any reliable translation will serve you well. This site quotes the King James Version, which is beautiful and widely loved, but its older English can be hard for new readers. Many people pair it with a modern translation such as the NIV, ESV, or NLT, or the NABRE for Catholic readers. Choose one you’ll actually read, and ask what your church uses.

Do I need to be baptized right away as a new Christian?

Jesus told his followers to baptize new disciples (Matthew 28:19), and in Acts new believers were usually baptized soon after they believed. Churches differ on timing and practice, and some baptize infants. Talk with a pastor or priest about the next step in your church. If you were baptized as a child, ask how that fits with your new commitment.

What if I don’t feel any different after becoming a Christian?

That’s common. Feelings rise and fall, even for people who have followed Jesus for decades. Your standing with God rests on his promises, not on how you feel on a given morning. Keep reading, praying, and gathering with other believers. Many people notice the change looking back over months: new desires, softer reactions, and a growing love for God and others.

What if I sin again after becoming a Christian?

You will, and so does every Christian. Sin is still serious, but it doesn’t undo God’s grace toward you. Confess it honestly, make things right with anyone you’ve hurt, and keep going. Philippians 1:6 assures believers that God will finish the good work he has begun in them, so a stumble is not the end of your story.

How do I find a good church as a new believer?

Look for a church where the Bible is taught clearly, Jesus is at the center, and people are genuinely welcoming. Visit a few times before deciding, and ask questions; a healthy church welcomes them and won’t pressure you. Online services can help, but in-person community is where you’ll be known and supported.
