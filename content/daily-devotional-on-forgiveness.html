A Daily Devotional on Forgiveness When the Hurt Is Still Raw

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

To forgive someone while the hurt is still raw, start by telling God the truth about what happened, then hand him your right to get even and ask for help with the next small step. You don’t have to feel forgiving today. Forgiveness is often a decision you make and then renew, and it never requires you to go back into danger.

Care note: If the person who hurt you is abusive, threatening, or controlling, your safety comes first. Forgiving them doesn’t mean staying, going back, or keeping quiet. Contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 (US) or local help, and call 911 if you are in immediate danger. A pastor or counselor can help with deep hurt. If you are thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US), or contact local emergency services.

Do I have to forgive right away?

No. Scripture gives you words for raw pain before it asks you to let anything go. David prayed this about someone he had trusted:

For it was not an enemy that reproached me; then I could have borne it: neither was it he that hated me that did magnify himself against me; then I would have hid myself from him: But it was thou, a man mine equal, my guide, and mine acquaintance. We took sweet counsel together, and walked unto the house of God in company.

Psalm 55:12–14

That isn’t a tidy prayer, and God kept it in the Bible anyway. Betrayal by a friend, a spouse, a parent, or a church leader cuts deeper than harm from a stranger, and you are allowed to say so. The psalms invite it: “pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us” (Psalm 62:8).

Even Joseph, whose forgiveness of his brothers is one of the great stories of Scripture, didn’t reach it in a day. More than twenty years passed between the day they sold him and the day he wept and told them who he was (Genesis 37–45). Being honest with God about the pain is part of forgiving, not a delay.

What does it mean to leave it to God?

Today’s reading is short. Read it slowly, twice.

Recompense to no man evil for evil. Provide things honest in the sight of all men. If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men. Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.

Romans 12:17–19

To “give place unto wrath” is to step aside and make room for God’s judgment. Paul isn’t saying what happened to you doesn’t matter. He is saying it matters so much that it belongs with the Judge who sees everything and gets it right. Forgiving means handing him the case. You stop trying to make the other person pay, and you trust him with the outcome.

Jesus did this in the worst moment imaginable. While he was being crucified, he prayed, “Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do” (Luke 23:34). He didn’t pretend the cross was fine. He brought the people doing it to his Father.

How do I forgive someone when I’m still angry?

Anger at a real wrong isn’t sin in itself (Ephesians 4:26). The question is what you do with it. Here is a way to pray today.

  1. Say all of it. Tell God what happened and how you feel, including the parts you would never say in church.
  2. Name what was taken. Trust, money, time, reputation, safety, a relationship you counted on. It’s hard to release a debt you haven’t named.
  3. Hand over the right to get even. Pray something like Lord, I give you my right to make them pay. You judge rightly, and I trust you with this.
  4. Take one protective step. Limit contact if you need to, stop rehearsing the story online or in your head, and tell one trusted person what’s going on.
  5. Pray again when the memory comes back. Paul says, “Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you” (Ephesians 4:31). Bitterness grows when a wound is fed. Each time you hand it back to God, you starve it a little.
  6. Pray for them, even briefly. Jesus told us to pray for those who mistreat us. A reluctant one-line prayer counts.

If anger keeps boiling over, see questions Christians ask about anger. For the wider teaching behind all this, read what the Bible says about love and forgiveness.

Does forgiving them mean I have to reconcile?

Not necessarily. Look again at Romans 12:18: “If it be possible, as much as lieth in you.” Paul admits that peace isn’t always possible, and that it doesn’t depend on you alone. Forgiveness can be given by one person. Reconciliation takes two, and it needs honesty, repentance, and trust rebuilt over time.

Joseph tested his brothers before he revealed himself, and he opened his heart only after Judah offered to become a slave in Benjamin’s place (Genesis 44:33). When the brothers later feared revenge, Joseph said, “ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good” (Genesis 50:20). He named the evil plainly and still chose kindness. If the person who hurt you is dangerous, forgiveness can be given from a distance, and reconciliation may not be safe or wise at all.

Father, you know what [name] did and how much it hurt. I won’t pretend it was small. I’m angry, and I’m tired of carrying this. I give you my right to make them pay. You see everything and you judge rightly, so I leave this with you. I don’t feel forgiving yet, but I choose to forgive, and I ask you to help my heart follow. Heal what was broken in me. Show me what wise limits look like, and keep me safe. Don’t let bitterness take root. Help me pray for them, even a little. Thank you that you have forgiven me in Christ. Amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I’ve forgiven them but it still hurts?

That’s normal. Forgiveness releases a debt, while healing mends a wound, and healing usually takes longer. “He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds” (Psalm 147:3). Keep bringing the pain to God. If memories of what happened are overwhelming or affect your sleep, work, or relationships, a Christian counselor or pastor can help you heal.

Should I tell the person I forgive them?

Sometimes. If they have apologized, telling them can open the door to repair. If they deny what happened, or contact with them isn’t safe, you can forgive before God without saying anything to them at all. Talk it through with a pastor or trusted friend first, and never reach out to someone who has been abusive just to deliver a message.

How do I forgive someone who isn’t sorry?

Forgiveness can be one-sided, even though reconciliation can’t. Jesus prayed for his executioners while they were still gambling for his clothes. You can release someone to God without an apology, and without pretending they’re sorry. You may still need limits, and you can keep praying for them to see what they did. Their repentance is God’s work, not yours.

Is it wrong to want justice?

No. God cares about justice, and Romans 12:19 hands vengeance to him rather than denying that wrongs should be answered. Revenge means taking punishment into your own hands. Reporting a crime, going to the authorities, telling church leaders about misconduct, or asking a court for protection is not revenge, and it can be a way of protecting others.

How do I forgive myself?

Scripture talks less about forgiving ourselves and more about receiving God’s forgiveness. If you have confessed and made amends where you can, God’s verdict stands even when your feelings argue with it. Keep returning to his promise instead of reopening the case. A pastor or counselor can help if guilt keeps pulling you back to the same failure long after you have confessed it.
