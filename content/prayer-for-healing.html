A Prayer for Healing

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

A prayer for healing brings your illness, injury, or pain honestly to God, asks him plainly to heal, and trusts him with the outcome. Scripture invites such asking and calls the church to pray with the sick (James 5:14-15). Prayer belongs alongside medical care, and slow or unanswered healing is never a measure of your faith.

Care note: Please keep following your doctor’s guidance while you pray, and don’t stop or change a treatment without talking to them first. For a medical emergency, call 911 in the US or your local emergency number. If illness has left you feeling hopeless or thinking about ending your life, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the US, or contact local emergency services outside the US.

A prayer for healing

Pray this for yourself, or put in the name of someone you love. Add the details only you know.

Lord Jesus, you touched the sick when others kept their distance, and you see every body that hurts. I bring you this illness, and I ask you plainly: please heal me. You know the diagnosis, the pain, the results I’m waiting for, and the fear that shows up at night.

Give wisdom and steady hands to my doctors, nurses, and everyone caring for me. Let the treatment do its work. Give patience and rest to my family. Where my body is weak, strengthen me for today. Where my heart is afraid, give me your peace.

Like you in the garden, I ask honestly for what I want, and I trust you with what I can’t control. Not as I will, but as thou wilt. Whatever comes, keep me close to you. In your name, amen.

How do I pray for healing for myself or someone I love?

The Gospels and the letter of James give a few simple patterns.

  1. Ask plainly. A man with leprosy knelt before Jesus and said, “If thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.” Mark adds that Jesus was “moved with compassion” and touched him (Mark 1:40-41). You don’t need special words. Say what is wrong and what you want.
  2. Carry someone who can’t pray. Four friends carried a paralyzed man to Jesus and lowered him through a roof. Mark writes, “When Jesus saw their faith,” he spoke to the man (Mark 2:5). You can bring a loved one to God when they are too sick, tired, or unsure to pray for themselves.
  3. Pray for the care as well as the cure. Name the surgeon, the medication, the date of the scan, the caregiver who is running on empty. God often works through the people and treatments you are praying for.
  4. End with trust. In Gethsemane Jesus asked for the cup to pass, then prayed, “nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt” (Matthew 26:39). That isn’t giving up. It’s placing an honest request in trustworthy hands.

The fifth chapter of James also tells the sick not to pray alone:

Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord: And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him.

James 5:14-15

Christians understand the promise in verse 15 in different ways, and the wider New Testament shows faithful believers who stayed sick. What the passage makes plain is that the sick should call on the church, and the church should come. Tell your pastor, priest, or small group what you are facing. The passage never gives anyone grounds to blame a sick person when healing is delayed.

A simple daily prayer rhythm for healing

Long illness and slow recovery can wear down your ability to pray. A few minutes a day is enough.

  1. Read one verse slowly, twice. Psalm 6:2 is a prayer you can borrow: “Have mercy upon me, O LORD; for I am weak: O LORD, heal me; for my bones are vexed.” In the Hebrew of Psalm 6:2, “vexed” means troubled or in anguish.
  2. Name today’s need in one sentence. Today’s pain, today’s appointment, today’s fear. Tomorrow can wait.
  3. Pray it aloud if you can. A whisper counts.
  4. Sit quietly for two minutes. You aren’t trying to produce anything. You are resting in God’s presence.
  5. Name one gift. A kind nurse, a text from a friend, an hour with less pain. Gratitude doesn’t deny what is hard. It keeps the hard things from being all you can see.

If you miss a day, begin again. On days when you can’t manage any of this, ask someone to read a psalm to you. For more Scripture to pray, see Bible verses for healing.

How do I keep praying when healing doesn’t come?

Paul asked God three times to remove his “thorn in the flesh.” The answer he received was that God’s grace was sufficient and his strength would show itself in Paul’s weakness (2 Corinthians 12:7-9). Paul didn’t pretend he hadn’t asked. He kept praying and learned to receive strength for the road instead of an exit from it.

Like Paul, keep asking. The psalmist whose words you borrowed above goes on to ask God, “how long?” (Psalm 6:3). Tell him when you are angry or disappointed. Your prayers may change shape over time, from “take this away” to “carry me through today,” and both are faithful.

If you or the person you prayed for has not been healed, it does not mean anyone lacked faith. Christians hope for the resurrection, when God will make our bodies whole for good. If you are grieving someone you prayed for, questions Christians ask about loss may help.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a short prayer for healing?

Try this: “Lord, you know what is wrong in my body. Please heal me, guide my doctors, and give me strength for today. I trust you with what I can’t control. Amen.” Pray it before appointments, during treatment, or when you wake in the night; swap in a loved one’s name to pray it for them. Short, honest prayers are real prayers.

What is a good prayer before surgery?

“Lord, I place this surgery in your hands. Give the surgeon and the whole team skill, clear minds, and steady hands. Calm my fear as I go in, protect me through the procedure, and be with my family while they wait. Help me recover well. Amen.” It can also help to ask a few friends to pray at the scheduled time.

Should I stop my medicine if I believe God will heal me?

No. Trusting God and following your doctor’s plan are not in competition. Never stop, skip, or change a medication or treatment without talking to your doctor, even if you believe you have been healed; let them confirm it. Many Christians give thanks to God both for answered prayer and for the medicine, surgery, or therapy that helped.

What does it mean to anoint the sick with oil?

James 5:14 describes church elders praying over a sick person and anointing them with oil in the Lord’s name. In the Bible oil was used to soothe wounds and to set people apart for God. Many Protestant churches still practice this through elders or pastors, and Catholic and Orthodox Christians receive it as the sacrament of the Anointing of the Sick. The oil is a sign of God’s care, not a formula.

Can I pray for emotional healing, not just physical?

Yes. Grief, trauma, a broken relationship, and memories that still hurt are all fair things to bring to God. Psalm 147:3 says he “healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.” Emotional healing is often slow and usually involves people too, such as a pastor, counselor, or trusted friend. Pray for it as specifically as you would for a physical illness.
