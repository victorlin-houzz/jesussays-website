A Short Prayer for Hope

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

A short prayer for hope can be one honest sentence: “Lord, I can’t see the way forward, but you can. Give me hope for today.” In the Bible, hope means confident waiting on God, not forced cheerfulness, so a brief prayer is enough. Below you’ll find a one-minute prayer, short prayers for specific moments, and the verses behind them.

Care note: If hopelessness has settled in for weeks, or you’ve had thoughts of not wanting to live, please reach out beyond prayer to a pastor, doctor, or counselor. In the US you can call or text 988 at any hour to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Outside the US, contact your local emergency services.

A one-minute prayer for hope

Father, I’m tired of hoping and being let down. I can’t make hope happen on my own today, so I’m asking you for it. You are the God of hope. Fill me with enough joy and peace to take the next step, and hold my future when I can’t see it. Let my hope rest on you, not on how things turn out this week. In Jesus’ name, amen.

Pray it in the morning before the day gets loud, or whenever discouragement hits. Say it slowly. The words don’t have to match your feelings yet.

What are short prayers for hope in hard moments?

Each of these takes a few seconds. Pick the one that fits and repeat it as often as you need.

  • When you’re waiting: Lord, I’m still waiting. Help me wait on you, not only on the outcome.
  • After a disappointment: That didn’t go the way I prayed. I’m hurt, and I’m still yours. Show me what’s next.
  • When the future scares you: I don’t know what’s coming, but you do. Let that be enough for today.
  • When you’re worn out: I have no strength left to hope. Lend me some of yours.
  • At night: I give you what I couldn’t fix today. Keep watch while I sleep.
  • For someone else: Lord, my friend is losing hope. Hold them close, and show me how to help.

Write one on a sticky note or set it as your lock screen so it’s there when you need it. If worry keeps you awake, this night prayer for sleep and anxiety may help.

What does the Bible say about hope?

Biblical hope isn’t wishful thinking. It is confidence in God’s character and promises, held while you wait for what you can’t yet see (Romans 8:24-25). These verses are good to pray back to God:

  1. Romans 15:13 — “Now the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that ye may abound in hope, through the power of the Holy Ghost.” Paul offers this as a blessing. Hope is something God gives. You don’t have to generate it yourself.
  2. Psalm 62:5 — “My soul, wait thou only upon God; for my expectation is from him.” “Expectation” here means what you are hoping for. David tells his own soul where to look.
  3. Lamentations 3:24 — “The LORD is my portion, saith my soul; therefore will I hope in him.” A “portion” was a share or inheritance. Written after Jerusalem fell, this is hope with almost everything else gone.
  4. Psalm 130:5 — “I wait for the LORD, my soul doth wait, and in his word do I hope.” In the Psalms, hoping and waiting go together. When nothing has changed yet, holding on to his word is still faith.
  5. Hebrews 6:19 — “Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast.” An anchor doesn’t stop the storm. It keeps the boat from drifting. “Stedfast” is the older spelling of steadfast.

What if I pray for hope and still feel hopeless?

Feelings often lag behind prayer, and that doesn’t mean God ignored you. Many psalms end with the writer still hurting. When you keep praying anyway, you are telling God where you want to stand, even before your emotions catch up.

Hope can also be borrowed for a while. Paul told believers to “Bear ye one another’s burdens” (Galatians 6:2), and sometimes that means letting a friend or your church hope on your behalf. Ask someone to pray for you by name this week. If the hopelessness is heavy and lasting, it may be more than discouragement. Talk with a pastor and a doctor or counselor, and see this prayer for depression and hopelessness.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the shortest prayer for hope?

Three words will do: “Lord, give hope.” You can also pray a line of Scripture, such as Psalm 39:7: “my hope is in thee.” Jesus praised a tax collector whose whole prayer was “God be merciful to me a sinner” (Luke 18:13). Length was never the point. A short prayer repeated through the day keeps turning your attention back to God.

Is Jeremiah 29:11 a promise for me?

In the KJV it reads, “For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.” God spoke it to Judeans exiled in Babylon, promising their return after seventy years (verse 10). It wasn’t a promise of quick relief or personal success. It does show God’s heart: he means good for his people, even in a long exile.

How do I pray for hope for someone else?

Pray for them by name and be specific: ask God to give them hope, rest, and good people nearby. Tell them you are praying, and ask what would help this week. You can pray Romans 15:13 over them or send it to them as a blessing. Hope often arrives through people, so a meal, a ride, or a visit can be part of the answer.

What does “hope maketh not ashamed” mean?

The phrase comes from Romans 5:5. “Maketh not ashamed” means that hope placed in God won’t leave you disappointed or embarrassed in the end. Paul gives the reason in the same verse: “the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost.” Christian hope rests on love God has already given, not on circumstances going your way.

Is it a sin to lose hope?

No. Feeling hopeless is a human experience, and Scripture admits it: “Hope deferred maketh the heart sick” (Proverbs 13:12). Long disappointment wears people down, and God doesn’t condemn you for that. He invites you to bring it to him honestly. The response the Bible models is prayer, lament, and leaning on others, not shame.
