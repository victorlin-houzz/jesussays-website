Morning Prayer for Protection

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

A morning prayer for protection places you, your family, and your day in God’s care before the day begins. David prayed, “in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee” (Psalm 5:3), and Scripture welcomes that prayer every day. It doesn’t promise a day without trouble. It promises that God will be with you in whatever comes.

What does the Bible say about praying for protection in the morning?

Jesus himself rose “a great while before day” to pray (Mark 1:35), and he taught his followers to ask, “deliver us from evil” (Matthew 6:13). These verses give you words for the start of the day.

  • Psalm 143:8 — “Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning; for in thee do I trust: cause me to know the way wherein I should walk; for I lift up my soul unto thee.” Protection in Scripture includes guidance. Ask God to show you the way as well as to guard it.
  • Psalm 121:7–8 — “The LORD shall preserve thee from all evil: he shall preserve thy soul. The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore.” “Preserve” means keep or guard. Pilgrims sang this on the road to Jerusalem. It covers your leaving, your coming home, and your whole life beyond today.
  • Proverbs 18:10 — “The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.” To run into God’s name is to call on him and rely on who he is. Morning prayer is a way of running there first.

Does Psalm 91 mean nothing bad will happen to me?

He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. I will say of the LORD, He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust.

Psalm 91:1–2

Psalm 91 is one of the Bible’s great songs of trust, and many Christians pray it every morning. Its pictures of a hiding place, a shadow, and a fortress are meant to steady you. But it is a song of trust, not a guarantee that faithful people will never be hurt, and Scripture itself shows why.

When Satan tempted Jesus in the wilderness, he quoted Psalm 91:11–12 and urged him to jump from the top of the temple, since angels would catch him. Jesus answered, “Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God” (Matthew 4:7). Using the psalm to demand safety is the very misuse Jesus refused.

Psalm 91 also expects hard days. In verse 15 God says, “I will be with him in trouble.” Paul wrote that “thrice I suffered shipwreck” (2 Corinthians 11:25), and Jesus told his disciples plainly, “In the world ye shall have tribulation” (John 16:33).

What God does promise is his presence in the valley (Psalm 23:4), his keeping of your soul, and a love that nothing, “neither death, nor life,” can separate you from (Romans 8:38–39). If harm comes after you have prayed, it doesn’t mean you prayed wrong or lacked faith.

How do I pray the armor of God in the morning?

Paul told believers to “put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil” (Ephesians 6:11). “Wiles” means schemes or tricks. A simple morning practice is to pray through each piece he names in verses 14–17:

  1. Truth. Paul pictures a soldier “having your loins girt about with truth,” his belt pulled tight. Ask God to keep you honest and clear-headed.
  2. Righteousness. He calls it “the breastplate of righteousness.” Ask for help to do right today, resting on Christ’s goodness rather than your own.
  3. Peace. Feet “shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace” are ready to move. Ask to carry peace into tense places.
  4. Faith. The shield of faith can “quench all the fiery darts of the wicked.” Name the fears or accusations you expect today and hand them to God.
  5. Salvation and Scripture. Ask God to guard your mind with “the helmet of salvation” and to bring his word to mind when you need it: “the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God.”

Paul wrote this as “an ambassador in bonds” (Ephesians 6:20), a prisoner. The armor didn’t keep him out of chains. It kept him standing.

How do I pray for protection each morning?

  1. Pray before you pick up your phone. Give God the first minute, before news and messages set the tone.
  2. Commit the day. “Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established” (Proverbs 16:3). Walk through your schedule and hand each part to him.
  3. Name your people. Pray for each person in your household by name, and for what they face today.
  4. Name your fears. Say what actually worries you. The God “that keepeth thee will not slumber” (Psalm 121:3).
  5. Pray, then act wisely. When enemies threatened Jerusalem, Nehemiah wrote, “we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night” (Nehemiah 4:9). Prayer and good sense go together: buckle up, lock the door, ask for help.

If someone is threatening or hurting you, please don’t rely on prayer alone. In the US, call 911 if you are in immediate danger, or call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233. Outside the US, contact local emergency services. If fear is heavy this morning, these Bible verses about peace and fear may help.

Lord, before this day begins, I place it in your hands. You are my refuge and my fortress; in you I trust. Watch over my going out and my coming in. Guard [names] wherever they go today, and keep us close to you.

Give me truth to stand on, faith when fear rises, and peace to carry into every conversation. Lead me not into temptation, and deliver me from evil. Give me wisdom to act well and courage to do what is right.

If trouble comes, be with me in it, as you have promised. Whatever this day holds, nothing can separate me from your love in Christ Jesus. In his name I pray, amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a short morning prayer for protection?

Try this as you get out of bed: Lord, you are my refuge and my fortress. Keep me, my family, and everyone I meet today in your care, and deliver us from evil. Amen. It draws on Psalm 91:2 and the Lord’s Prayer. You can repeat a short prayer like this through the day, in the car, before a meeting, or before a hard conversation.

How do I pray for protection over my children?

Pray for each child by name and be specific: their safety, friendships, choices, and faith. Job rose early to offer a sacrifice for each of his children, concerned for their hearts before God (Job 1:5). Ask God for wisdom as a parent too, since he often protects children through the adults who watch over them. Psalm 121:8 is a good verse to pray as they head out the door.

If I prayed for protection and something bad happened, did God fail me?

No. Scripture never promises believers a life without harm, and suffering after prayer isn’t proof that God stopped caring or that your faith was too small. Jesus prayed in Gethsemane and still went to the cross. God promises to be with you in trouble and to hold you through it. Bring your grief and questions to him honestly, and to a pastor or friend who can walk with you.

What does “deliver us from evil” mean?

“Deliver us from evil” is the last request in the Lord’s Prayer (Matthew 6:13). The original Greek can mean evil in general or the evil one, the devil, and many modern translations choose the second. Either way, Jesus teaches us to ask God each day to rescue us from sin, from the devil’s schemes, and from harm. It is a prayer for the soul as much as the body.

Is it okay to pray Psalm 91 every day?

Yes. Christians have prayed Psalm 91 for centuries, and praying it daily can steady your heart. Pray it as trust in God’s character rather than as a formula that guarantees safety. Reading the whole psalm, including verse 15, “I will be with him in trouble,” keeps its promises in their true shape: God’s presence and care in every circumstance.
