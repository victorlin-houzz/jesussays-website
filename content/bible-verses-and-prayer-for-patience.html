Bible Verses and a Prayer for Patience

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

The Bible describes patience as steady trust in God while you wait and gentle endurance with other people. Psalm 37:7 says, "Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him," and Galatians 5:22 lists longsuffering as part of the fruit of the Spirit. That means patience is something God grows in you as you keep asking him for it.

What does the Bible say about waiting on God?

Most of the Bible's words about patience were written to people in real hardship: exiles, persecuted believers, people watching the wicked get ahead. That context keeps these verses from sounding like slogans.

  • Psalm 37:7 — "Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him: fret not thyself because of him who prospereth in his way, because of the man who bringeth wicked devices to pass." David wrote for people watching dishonest schemes succeed. To fret is to stew and burn inside. Patience starts by handing that burning over to God.
  • Isaiah 40:31 — "But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint." These words went to a weary people who felt God had lost track of them (Isaiah 40:27). Waiting on him is where strength gets renewed, including strength for ordinary, plodding days.
  • Lamentations 3:25–26 — "The LORD is good unto them that wait for him, to the soul that seeketh him. It is good that a man should both hope and quietly wait for the salvation of the LORD." This was written among the ruins of Jerusalem. Quiet waiting here doesn't deny grief; the rest of Lamentations 3 is full of lament.
  • James 1:3–4 — "Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing." In the KJV, "patience" often means endurance and "perfect" means mature, which you can see in the Greek words of James 1:4. The wait itself is doing something in you.
  • James 5:7–8 — "Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain. Be ye also patient; stablish your hearts: for the coming of the Lord draweth nigh." A farmer can't hurry the rain, but he keeps tending his field. "Stablish" means strengthen or steady.

What does the Bible say about patience with other people?

The KJV often calls this kind of patience longsuffering: bearing with slowness, irritation, and wrongs without striking back. Paul lists it among the fruit of the Spirit, and in 1 Corinthians 13 it is the first thing he says about love.

With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love; Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

Ephesians 4:2–3

"Forbearing" means putting up with. Paul assumes believers will test each other's patience, and he asks them to carry that strain in love because unity matters to God. In practice it looks like letting a small irritation go, giving someone another try, or waiting to reply until you're calm. Proverbs 15:18 notes that the person who is slow to anger calms a quarrel. If your impatience often turns into anger, see what the Bible says about anger.

Is patience the same as doing nothing?

No. Biblical patience is active. Hebrews 10:36 tells believers they need patience so that, after doing the will of God, they will receive what he promised. Waiting and obeying happen together. The farmer in James 5 still plows and plants, and Jesus told a parable so that people would keep praying and not lose heart (Luke 18:1).

So while you wait, keep doing the next faithful thing. Send the application, go to the appointment, show up for your family, keep praying. Patience means you stop trying to force an outcome that belongs to God. You still act wherever you can.

How do I pray for patience?

Patience usually grows through many small, honest prayers. A simple pattern you can repeat each day:

  1. Tell God how the wait feels. Frustrated, tired, scared. The Psalms show that this kind of honesty belongs in prayer.
  2. Name the timeline you've been holding. The date you expected, the outcome you pictured. Hand it to him specifically.
  3. Ask for the fruit as well as the answer. Ask God to grow endurance, peace, and trust in you while you wait (James 1:4).
  4. Pray again tomorrow. Coming back with the same request is persistence, which Jesus commended in the parable of the widow and the judge (Luke 18:1–8).

Father, I'm tired of waiting, and you know how much I want this settled. I give you my timeline and my plans. Give me a quiet heart that trusts your goodness, strength for today, and gentleness with the people around me. Grow in me the patience I can't produce on my own, and help me keep doing what is right while I wait on you. In Jesus' name, amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it dangerous to pray for patience?

People sometimes joke that praying for patience brings more trouble. The Bible doesn't teach that God punishes the request. James 1:3–4 does say trials produce endurance, so God may grow patience through what you're already facing. You can ask freely: James 1:5 says God gives wisdom generously to anyone who asks, without scolding them for asking.

What did Jesus say about patience?

Warning his disciples about coming persecution, Jesus said, "In your patience possess ye your souls" (Luke 21:19), meaning steady endurance would preserve them. In the parable of the sower, he described the good soil as people who hear the word, hold on to it, and bear fruit with patience (Luke 8:15). For Jesus, patience is faithfulness that lasts.

Is impatience a sin?

Feeling impatient is part of being human, and the Psalms are full of people asking God how long they must wait. Impatience becomes sin when it turns into harsh words, shortcuts God hasn't given, or mistreating people. When that happens, confess it and ask for help. God is slow to anger with us (Psalm 103:8), and he can make us more like him.

What is the difference between patience and longsuffering?

In the KJV, patience usually means endurance through trials or delay, while longsuffering means bearing with people and holding back anger for a long time. Both grow from trust in God. Scripture also uses longsuffering for God himself, who holds back judgment to give people time to repent (2 Peter 3:9).

How long does God make us wait?

Scripture doesn't give a timetable, and some believers waited for years: Abraham and Sarah for a son, Joseph in prison, Israel in exile. A long wait isn't a sign that God has forgotten you. Keep praying honestly, seek counsel from wise people about decisions in front of you, and trust that God's timing is shaped by his love.
