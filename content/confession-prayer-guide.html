Confession Prayer Guide: Praying Psalm 51

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

To pray a confession prayer, tell God plainly what you did, ask for mercy on the basis of his character rather than your record, and receive the forgiveness he promises in 1 John 1:9. Psalm 51, David’s prayer after Nathan confronted him, gives you the pattern and even the words: mercy, honesty, cleansing, a new heart, and restored joy.

What does Psalm 51 teach about confession?

David wrote Psalm 51 after his adultery with Bathsheba and his arranged killing of her husband, Uriah (2 Samuel 11–12). For months he kept it hidden. When Nathan exposed it, David finally said, “I have sinned against the LORD” (2 Samuel 12:13). Psalm 51 is the prayer that came out of that moment, and five lines show its shape.

  • Psalm 51:1 — “Have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy lovingkindness: according unto the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out my transgressions.” David starts with God’s steadfast love, not with promises to do better. Confession stands on who God is.
  • Psalm 51:3 — “For I acknowledge my transgressions: and my sin is ever before me.” No excuses, no blaming. Naming the sin plainly is where relief begins.
  • Psalm 51:10 — “Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.” The Hebrew word for “create” in this verse is the one used in Genesis 1:1. David asks for something only God can make: new desires, not just a clean record.
  • Psalm 51:12 — “Restore unto me the joy of thy salvation; and uphold me with thy free spirit.” Confession aims at restored closeness and joy. It was never meant to end in self-punishment.
  • Psalm 51:17 — “The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.” “Contrite” means crushed with sorrow. God isn’t asking for a performance, only for honesty.

One more line deserves attention. In verse 4 David prays, “Against thee, thee only, have I sinned,” even though he had terribly wronged Bathsheba and Uriah. He isn’t denying the harm he did to them. He is saying that every sin is finally against God. A full confession also takes in the people we have hurt, and forgiveness doesn’t always remove the consequences, as David learned.

How do I pray a confession prayer, step by step?

  1. Start with God’s mercy. Find somewhere quiet and pray Psalm 51:1 slowly, or simply say, Lord, have mercy on me. You are coming to a Father, not a prosecutor.
  2. Examine your conscience. Ask God to show you what is true. Think back over the last day or week: what you did, what you said, what you left undone, and who was affected. Writing it down can keep you honest.
  3. Name it plainly. Say what happened in simple words, and stop before the explanations start. I lied to my sister. I was harsh with my son. I took what wasn’t mine. Specific confession brings specific relief.
  4. Ask for a changed heart. Pray Psalm 51:10. You are asking God to change what you want, not only to wipe the slate.
  5. Receive forgiveness. Rest on the promise below, whether or not you feel different yet.
  6. Make it right where you can. Apologize, repay, tell the truth, or remove what keeps tripping you up. If the same temptation keeps coming back, these verses and a prayer for temptation can help you plan ahead.

If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

1 John 1:9

John says God is “faithful and just” to forgive. He forgives because he keeps his word, and because Jesus Christ has already dealt with our sin (1 John 2:1–2). You receive that forgiveness by trusting him, not by feeling sorry enough. If shame hangs on after you confess, the confession prayer after failure looks at that more closely.

Should I confess to God alone, or to a priest or pastor?

Christians answer this differently, so it helps to know what your own church teaches.

Catholic and Orthodox Christians receive the sacrament of confession, also called Reconciliation or Penance, with a priest, and the Catholic Church asks the faithful to bring serious sin to the sacrament (Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1457). If that is your tradition, praying Psalm 51 and examining your conscience is a good way to prepare. It goes with the sacrament and doesn’t take its place.

Many Protestant Christians confess directly to God in private prayer and in a shared prayer of confession during worship. Some Lutheran and Anglican churches also offer private confession with a pastor. In every tradition, Scripture calls believers to be honest with one another:

Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.

James 5:16

Whatever your church, don’t carry a heavy sin alone. A priest, pastor, or mature believer can pray with you and help you take the next step.

Should I pray a confession prayer before fasting?

Many Christians do, especially on Ash Wednesday, during Lent, or before a personal day of prayer. In Scripture, fasting and turning back to God go together. Through the prophet Joel, God called his people:

Therefore also now, saith the LORD, turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning: And rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto the LORD your God: for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil.

Joel 2:12–13

Tearing your clothes was a public sign of grief. God asked for a torn heart instead. (“Repenteth him of the evil” means God is willing to hold back the judgment he had warned about.) Isaiah 58 adds that a fast means little while we keep mistreating people, so a confession before fasting should cover how you have treated others as well as your private habits.

Before the fast begins, pray through Psalm 51, name what stands between you and God, receive his forgiveness, and tell him plainly why you are fasting. When hunger comes, let it turn you back to prayer. The fast doesn’t pay for anything; forgiveness is already given in Christ. If you have a medical condition, take medication, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating, talk with your doctor before fasting from food, and ask your pastor or priest about other ways to keep the fast.

Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love. I come to you without excuses. I confess that I [name it plainly]. I am sorry, for the wrong itself and for the people it has hurt.

Wash me and make me clean. Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me. Thank you that you are faithful and just to forgive me through Jesus Christ.

Show me what I need to make right, and give me the courage to do it. Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and keep me close to you today. Amen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do I still feel guilty after I confess?

Feelings often lag behind the facts. Forgiveness rests on God’s promise, not on how you feel an hour later. Lingering guilt can be a nudge to make something right with another person. If you confess the same forgiven sin over and over and never find peace, talk with a pastor or priest, and consider a Christian counselor. That kind of anxious, repeating guilt is common, and help is available.

Do I have to remember every sin to be forgiven?

No. 1 John 1:9 promises cleansing “from all unrighteousness,” which reaches past what you can recall. Confess what you know honestly and specifically, and trust God with the rest. The goal is truthfulness, not a perfect inventory. If you are Catholic and preparing for the sacrament, your priest can guide you on what to bring and how to examine your conscience.

Is confession the same as repentance?

Confession and repentance belong together. Proverbs 28:13 says, “He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.” Confessing is naming the sin; forsaking it is turning away and walking a different direction. Confession starts repentance, and repentance shows up in changed choices, repaired relationships, and help sought for patterns you can’t break alone.

How often should Christians pray a prayer of confession?

Scripture sets no schedule, but Jesus put a request for forgiveness in the Lord’s Prayer, which points to a regular habit rather than a crisis-only one. Many Christians confess briefly at the end of each day, and many churches include a prayer of confession in weekly worship. Catholics also receive the sacrament of confession regularly, as their church teaches.

Can fasting itself be a form of repentance?

In the Bible, fasting often went with repentance. Joel called Israel to return “with fasting,” and the people of Nineveh fasted when Jonah warned them (Jonah 3:5). Fasting doesn’t pay for sin or earn forgiveness. It is a bodily way of saying you are serious about turning to God, and it only means something when a changed heart goes with it.
