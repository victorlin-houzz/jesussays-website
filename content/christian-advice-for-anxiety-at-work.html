Christian Advice for Anxiety at Work and Burnout

By the Jesus Says teamAI-assisted, reviewed by VictorUpdated September 29, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Christian advice for anxiety at work starts with where your worth comes from: you work for the Lord, not for a performance review (Colossians 3:23–24). From there, Scripture gives practical help: commit each task to God, pray short prayers during the day, take real rest, and ask for help before you wear out, as Moses had to learn.

Care note: Prayer and Scripture support care but don't replace it. If work anxiety or burnout is affecting your sleep, health, or ability to function, talk with a doctor or counselor. For harassment, discrimination, or unsafe conditions, contact HR, a legal advisor, or the appropriate authority. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the US, or contact local emergency services.

What does the Bible say about stress at work?

Work isn't a punishment. God gave Adam a garden to tend before anything went wrong (Genesis 2:15). But Scripture is honest that work can become heavy, anxious, and endless. These passages speak to both sides.

  • Colossians 3:23–24 — “And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men; Knowing that of the Lord ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance: for ye serve the Lord Christ.” Paul first wrote this to bondservants, people with far less control over their work than most employees today. If their labor was seen and rewarded by Christ, so is yours. A manager's opinion matters, but it doesn't get the final word on your worth. And this verse is never a reason to accept mistreatment.
  • Proverbs 16:3 — “Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established.” The Hebrew word for “commit” pictures rolling a load onto someone else. Handing your work to God at the start of the day steadies your thoughts, which is exactly where work anxiety lives.
  • Exodus 18:17–18 — “And Moses' father in law said unto him, The thing that thou doest is not good. Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone.” Moses was settling every dispute for a whole nation, from morning to evening. Jethro saw what Moses couldn't, and the fix was practical: share the load with capable people (Exodus 18:21–22). Delegating and asking for help can be the faithful choice.
  • Mark 6:31 — “And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.” The disciples had just returned from a busy mission, and Jesus took them away to rest. Their rest was interrupted by a crowd, which may sound familiar, but rest was still his plan for them.
  • Matthew 11:28–30 — “Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.” A yoke is a work tool, so this is a work image. Jesus offers to share the load and set the pace, and he describes himself as “meek and lowly in heart,” the opposite of a harsh boss.
  • Ecclesiastes 4:6 — “Better is an handful with quietness, than both the hands full with travail and vexation of spirit.” The Preacher had watched people grab for more with both hands and come away with only “vexation of spirit.” Sometimes the faithful choice is less: fewer commitments, a slower season, one handful held quietly.

How can a Christian handle anxiety at work day to day?

  1. Commit the day before you open email. Take one minute to pray Proverbs 16:3 over your calendar. Then name the two or three things that truly matter today, so the loudest request doesn't automatically become the most important one.
  2. Use quick prayers in the moment. When the Persian king noticed that Nehemiah, his cupbearer, looked troubled, Nehemiah was “very sore afraid” (Nehemiah 2:2). Asked what he wanted, he writes, “So I prayed to the God of heaven,” and then gave his answer (Nehemiah 2:4–5). The prayer took seconds. You can do the same before a hard meeting or a tense email: Lord, give me wisdom and a steady voice.
  3. Separate the result from your worth. When a project fails or a review stings, say the true sentence: “This work fell short,” not “I am a failure.” Learn what you can, make it right where you need to, and bring the rest to God.
  4. Take the breaks you're given. Eat lunch away from your desk when you can. Keep a Sabbath rhythm, a regular stretch of time each week when you stop, even if your schedule means it isn't Sunday. Rest is one way of trusting God with what you leave undone.
  5. Tell one person. Burnout grows in isolation. Tell a spouse, friend, pastor, or mentor that you're running on empty. Galatians 6:2 asks believers to bear one another's burdens, and that includes yours.

Is it wrong for a Christian to set limits at work?

No. Limits are built into creation. God worked six days and rested on the seventh, and he commanded rest for the whole household, servants and animals included (Exodus 20:9–10). A boundary around your evenings, a “no” to one more project, or an honest conversation about workload doesn't make you lazy. You're a creature with a body, and it's God, not your output, who holds the world together.

Diligence still matters. Scripture praises honest, careful work and warns against laziness. The aim is faithful work at a pace you can sustain, done for the Lord, with a Sabbath shape to your week. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a short prayer for anxiety at work?

Try this: Lord, establish the work of my hands today, and let me do it for you. It echoes Psalm 90:17 and takes about ten seconds. Pray it before a meeting, while a file uploads, or in the parking lot. Work anxiety builds in small moments, and each short prayer hands one of those moments back to God.

Is it a sin to be stressed at work?

Stress itself isn't sin. It's a normal response to deadlines, pressure, and responsibility, and faithful people in Scripture felt it. It can lead to sin when fear pushes you to lie, cut corners, or treat people harshly, or when work becomes the thing you trust most. Bring the stress to God honestly, and let it show you what may need to change.

Should a Christian quit a job that causes anxiety?

Sometimes, but not in a panic. Pray, and talk it through with people who know you well; Proverbs 15:22 says plans are established through many counsellors. Look at what is actually driving the anxiety: the workload, one person, the work itself, or something outside the job. Some situations can be fixed. Others are a sign it's time to move on, carefully and with practical needs in view.

How should a Christian deal with a difficult boss?

Romans 12:18 asks you to live peaceably with everyone as far as it depends on you. You're responsible for your own conduct, not for changing your boss. Stay honest and respectful, keep notes on real problems, and seek wise counsel. Living peaceably never means accepting harassment or abuse; those belong with HR, legal help, or the proper authorities.

What does the Bible say about burnout?

The word isn't in the Bible, but the experience is. After a draining season, the prophet Elijah collapsed under a tree and asked God to take his life. God didn't lecture him. An angel let him sleep, woke him to eat, and said the journey was too great for him (1 Kings 19:4–7). If you feel like Elijah did, start with rest and help, and if you have thoughts of dying, call or text 988 now.
