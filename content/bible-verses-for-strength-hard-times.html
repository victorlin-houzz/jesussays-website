Bible Verses for Strength in Hard Times

By the Jesus Says teamUpdated September 28, 2026Scripture: King James Version

Bible verses for strength in hard times include Isaiah 40:29-31, 2 Corinthians 12:9, Psalm 46:1, Psalm 73:26, and Habakkuk 3:18-19. They describe strength God gives to the weary, not grit you have to summon on your own, and they are honest that hard seasons can last. Read slowly, one at a time, they can help you get through today.

Care note: If your hard season involves illness, grief, a job loss, or a crisis at home, Scripture can steady you, and it also works best alongside people who can help: a pastor, doctor, counselor, or trusted friend. If you are thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US, or your local emergency number elsewhere.

What are the best Bible verses for strength in hard times?

Each verse below is quoted from the King James Version, with a note on its setting.

  1. Isaiah 40:29-31 — “He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength. Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall: But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.” Isaiah wrote to exiles who felt God had lost track of them (Isaiah 40:27). Notice where the list ends: walking. Some days, walking without fainting is the answer to prayer.
  2. 2 Corinthians 12:9 — “My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.” Paul had asked God three times to remove a painful “thorn in the flesh.” This was the reply. God didn’t make him strong enough to stop needing help; he promised strength that shows up precisely where Paul was weak.
  3. Psalm 46:1 — “God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.” “Very present” means close at hand, right in the middle of the trouble. The psalm goes on to imagine the earth giving way and the mountains sliding into the sea, and it still finds God near.
  4. Psalm 73:26 — “My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever.” Asaph admits that both his body and his resolve are failing. He doesn’t claim to be strong. He says God is.
  5. Habakkuk 3:18-19 — “Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation. The LORD God is my strength, and he will make my feet like hinds’ feet, and he will make me to walk upon mine high places.” The verse before lists failed crops and empty stalls, a total loss of livelihood. “Hinds’ feet” are the sure feet of a deer on rocky ground. The strength promised here is footing on hard terrain, not a way around it.

Does Philippians 4:13 mean I can do anything?

This is one of the most quoted strength verses, and it reads differently in context:

I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.

Philippians 4:12-13

Paul wrote this while imprisoned, thanking a church for a gift. “All things” means getting through every circumstance, hunger and plenty, need and abundance, with contentment. It isn’t a promise that you will win the game or reach every goal. It is a promise that Christ can sustain you in whatever situation you are in, which is better news when life is hard.

How do I find strength when I have nothing left?

In Scripture, strength usually comes through ordinary means, and often through other people.

  1. Accept help. When Moses’ arms grew too heavy to lift during a battle, Aaron and Hur held them up, one on each side (Exodus 17:12). Let someone hold up your arms: a friend, your church, a counselor.
  2. Rest without guilt. Jesus told his exhausted disciples, “Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while” (Mark 6:31). Tiredness is not a spiritual failure.
  3. Carry one verse into each day. Read it aloud in the morning before you check the news or your messages, and come back to it when you hit a wall.
  4. Lower the bar. Isaiah 40:31 ends with walking. In a hard season, doing the next right thing is enough.
  5. Tell God the truth. “I can’t do this” is an honest way to begin a prayer. Paul’s strength began with admitting weakness.

What if the hard time doesn’t end soon?

Scripture rarely promises short suffering. It promises company through it. Peter wrote to believers facing a long trial:

But the God of all grace, who hath called us unto his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after that ye have suffered a while, make you perfect, stablish, strengthen, settle you.

1 Peter 5:10

“A while” can feel very long, and Peter doesn’t pretend otherwise. “Stablish” and “settle” both mean to make firm, like a house set on a foundation. A long season isn’t evidence that God is absent or punishing you. Lament is allowed. Keep praying honestly and keep leaning on others. If your hard time is grief, see questions Christians ask about loss; if it is work and money, see Bible verses for job loss and unemployment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a short prayer for strength?

Try this: “Lord, I’m weak and I can’t carry this alone. Give me strength for today, not the whole road. Hold me up, and send people to help. Amen.” Pray it in the morning or right before a hard moment. Jesus taught us to ask for daily bread, and strength often comes the same way: enough for one day at a time.

Which psalm is good for strength?

Psalm 46 is for when everything feels like it is shaking. Psalm 18 opens with “I will love thee, O LORD, my strength” and suits days you feel under attack. Psalm 27 closes with “be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart.” Psalm 121 is a traveler’s psalm that asks where help comes from and answers, “from the LORD.” Read one aloud as a prayer.

What does “wait upon the LORD” mean in Isaiah 40:31?

The Hebrew word behind “wait” carries a sense of hopeful expectation, like watching for someone you know is coming. It isn’t passive resignation, and it isn’t frantic effort either. Waiting on the Lord means trusting him, looking to him, and continuing to walk while you wait. Isaiah says that kind of waiting is where renewed strength is found.

Is it a lack of faith to feel weak?

No. Jesus himself was so distressed in Gethsemane that “there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him” (Luke 22:43). Paul said he would “glory in my infirmities” because Christ’s power rested on him there. Admitting weakness is honest, and asking for help is humility. Neither one means your faith is failing.

What Bible verse can I send a friend going through a hard time?

Keep it short. Isaiah 41:10 works well: “Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God.” So does Deuteronomy 33:27: “underneath are the everlasting arms.” Add a line in your own words and something practical, like “Can I bring dinner Thursday?” A verse lands best when it comes with presence.
